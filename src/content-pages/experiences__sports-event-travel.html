Common Questions

Sports & Events Travel — FAQ

Questions about booking sports travel, event access, hospitality packages, and how to combine major events with a broader trip.

How far in advance do I need to book for major events?+
For marquee events — Formula 1 races, Wimbledon, the Masters, World Cup matches — 6–18 months in advance is standard for the best access. Some packages become available on shorter notice when earlier bookings fall through, but premium seating, the best hospitality suites, and prime hotel locations require early commitment.
Can you get access to sold-out events?+
Often, yes. I work with operators who hold pre-allocated hospitality packages for events that may be technically sold out to the general public. This includes premium viewing, dedicated hospitality, catering, and transfers — it's different from buying a resold ticket. Availability and pricing vary significantly by event and timing.
What types of sports travel do you specialize in?+
Golf travel is a strong category — links courses in Scotland and Ireland, private club access, resort courses in the US and Caribbean. Formula 1 hospitality packages. Tennis at Wimbledon and Roland Garros. The Masters and US Open. Rugby and football internationally. If there's a sport you're passionate about, the question is usually access and logistics, not availability.
Can I combine event attendance with general travel?+
This is the standard structure for most sports trips. A Formula 1 race in Monaco pairs naturally with a few days on the Côte d'Azur or a day trip into Italy. The Masters pairs with Charleston or Savannah. Wimbledon anchors a London trip. I design the broader itinerary to complement the event, not just move you to and from the venue.
What should a hospitality package include?+
At minimum: event access, a dedicated viewing area or hospitality suite, meals and premium drinks, and an event program. Better packages add private transfers, paddock or backstage access, celebrity appearances, and pre-event experiences. I review package inclusions carefully against price before recommending any specific product — the premium can vary as much as the product does.
Is golf travel only for expert golfers?+
No. Many of the world's best golf destinations — Ireland, Scotland, Portugal — welcome all skill levels. The experience of playing a historic links course in County Clare or a cliff-side course in the Algarve is compelling regardless of handicap. I match courses to playing level and ensure expectations are set correctly so no one ends up on a course they're not ready for.
How do I choose between watching an event and playing it?+
Both are worth doing, and many clients do both on the same trip. Attending a major tournament as a spectator is a different experience from playing the course yourself. The Open at St Andrews and a round on the Old Course in the same week, for instance, is entirely achievable with the right planning.
What's the best way to handle travel insurance for event trips?+
Event trips carry higher cancellation stakes than standard travel — if you can't attend the event, the entire trip's purpose may be lost. I recommend policies with "cancel for any reason" (CFAR) riders for event travel, and I advise on booking event access through packages that include their own cancellation protection where possible.
