Why Safari Travel

The Difference Is Almost
Entirely the Guide

Peak timing is by country, not by continent, and the 4 planned most often diverge. Kenya and Tanzania are best July through October for the Great Migration; Botswana peaks May to October, when the dry season concentrates game on water; green season, December to March, costs less and delivers calving and birdlife. Camp choice — its size, its concession, its guides — outranks the month every time.

A safari built around the right camp with the right guide is one of the most extraordinary experiences in travel. A safari built around the wrong ones is a long, dusty week of disappointment. The difference is almost never reflected in the brochure.

We recommend a short list of camps, and we hold a direct relationship with the operator behind every one of them. We know which lodges treat their guides as employees and which treat them as partners. We know which conservancies enforce vehicle limits and which silently let safari trucks pile up around a leopard sighting. Those distinctions are the trip.

The questions we ask before placing you in a camp are not the questions most agents think to ask. How long has the head guide been there? What's the camp's policy on off-roading? How many vehicles do they put on a single sighting? Get those answers right, and the rest of the trip takes care of itself.

Calving season in the south. Lush landscapes, dramatic skies, fewer guests, and meaningfully lower rates. Best for photographers and birders.

Common Questions

Safari & Wildlife — FAQ

The questions we hear most often when planning a safari. More detail on the full FAQ page.

Peak timing varies by destination. Kenya and Tanzania's Masai Mara and Serengeti are best July–October for the Great Migration. Botswana peaks May–October during the dry season, when wildlife concentrates around water sources. South Africa is excellent year-round. The "best" month depends on what you most want to see.

Each delivers a different experience. Kenya and Tanzania are defined by open savanna and the Great Migration. Botswana is less visited, more remote, and extraordinary for specialist wildlife — wild dogs, large elephant herds, pristine waterways. South Africa is the most accessible first safari: strong infrastructure, no malaria in private reserves, excellent luxury camps at a higher quality-to-cost ratio.

A tented camp is typically a permanent canvas structure on a platform — intimate, immersive, with the sounds of the bush all around you. A lodge uses fixed-structure rooms or suites. Both can be equally luxurious. Many of the world's finest safari properties are tented camps. The distinction is about atmosphere and proximity to nature, not quality.

Less than most people expect. Safari consists primarily of game drives in 4x4 vehicles, typically at dawn and dusk when wildlife is most active. Walking safaris, where available, add more exertion. Most itineraries are accessible to any reasonable fitness level, including older travelers and families.

Yes, but age policies vary by camp. Many top Botswana camps require children to be at least 12 — game drives run unpredictably and young children can limit what activities are available for the vehicle. South Africa, Kenya, and certain family-specific camps accommodate children as young as 5 or 6 with dedicated programs. I match families to properties that genuinely welcome children.

Depends on the destination. South Africa generally requires no vaccinations for most travelers. Kenya, Tanzania, and Botswana recommend or require malaria prophylaxis; yellow fever documentation may be required based on transit countries. I provide a full health and entry brief for every itinerary and recommend consulting a travel health clinic 6–8 weeks before departure.

A minimum of five nights in the bush makes sense. This allows time for multiple game drives and the chance to settle into the daily rhythm of the bush. Seven to ten nights is the sweet spot for a first safari. Under four nights tends to feel rushed and produces fewer meaningful encounters.

Top-tier camps in Botswana start around $700–$1,500 per person per night, all-inclusive. A meaningful luxury safari of 8–10 days typically runs $8,000–$20,000+ per person depending on destination, timing, and camp selection. South Africa offers a higher quality-to-cost ratio. Pricing includes accommodation, meals, game activities, and often internal flights.
