The Region

The Greatest Wildlife
Theater on Earth

The Great Migration is a continuous cycle, not a single event. Roughly 1.5 million wildebeest, accompanied by hundreds of thousands of zebra and gazelle, move through the Serengeti and Masai Mara in a clockwise loop that takes a full year to complete. Calving begins on the southern Serengeti plains in January and February. The herds build north through June. The river crossings — the Mara River, with its crocodiles and chaos — peak between July and October. By November, the cycle begins again. Being in the right place at the right week requires knowing exactly where in that cycle the herds are, and choosing camps positioned for it.

Kenya and Tanzania sit on opposite sides of an invisible border that the wildebeest ignore entirely. The Masai Mara on the Kenyan side and the Serengeti on the Tanzanian side form one continuous ecosystem — the greatest concentration of large mammals on the planet. A combined itinerary covering both countries in twelve to fourteen days captures the full range of East African wildlife without redundancy. But the countries are not interchangeable. Kenya's private conservancies bordering the Mara national reserve offer something the Tanzanian parks cannot: absolute exclusivity, with vehicle counts limited to a handful per sighting and guests capped at twenty-four per camp.

The guide is the variable that matters most. A guide who has worked the Mara ecosystem for fifteen years reads animal behavior differently than one who arrived last season. The difference shows in where the vehicle is positioned before the leopard appears, and in how long the camp stays at a sighting before moving on. That level of reading is only available at camps that hire and retain the right people — a short list that takes years to develop.

Six Ecosystems Worth Planning Properly

Destination Photography · Masai Mara Conservancies
Kenya
Masai Mara Conservancies
The private conservancies bordering the Mara national reserve — Olare Motorogi, Mara North, Naboisho, Ol Kinyei — operate under a fundamentally different model than the park. No vehicles from the reserve enter the conservancy land. Guest counts are capped at twenty-four per property. A single vehicle may stay at a sighting for as long as the guide decides, without the pressure of other vehicles rotating in. The wildebeest crossings of the Mara River between July and October are the most dramatic wildlife spectacle on earth, and being positioned correctly for them requires exactly this kind of access. Night drives, bush walks, and off-road driving are permitted — none of which are allowed in the national reserve itself.

Destination Photography · Serengeti
Tanzania
Serengeti
Tanzania's Serengeti spans from the southern calving plains near Ndutu — where the wildebeest give birth in January and February in numbers that make the landscape look like it's moving — to the northern Serengeti, where the crossing drama mirrors the Mara. Mobile camps that reposition seasonally to follow the herds offer the most precisely timed access. Fixed camps in different zones capture different parts of the same story. The Serengeti's scale — fifteen thousand square kilometers — makes camp location the decisive planning variable.

Destination Photography · Amboseli
Kenya
Amboseli
The elephant herds of Amboseli move against the backdrop of Kilimanjaro in a composition that has no equivalent elsewhere in Africa. The park contains some of the best-studied elephant families on the continent — four decades of continuous observation by the Amboseli Elephant Research Project means the guides here understand individual animals and their relationships at a level that adds a dimension of context unavailable anywhere else. The Big Life Foundation's conservation work extending into the surrounding community lands shapes the experience directly.

Destination Photography · Ngorongoro Crater
Tanzania
Ngorongoro Crater
The world's largest intact volcanic caldera — twenty-two kilometers across, with walls that rise six hundred meters above the crater floor — is a self-contained ecosystem that has been isolated from the surrounding landscape long enough to develop its own dynamics. The Big Five density per square mile is among the highest on the continent. The black rhino population, though small, offers some of the most reliable rhino sightings in East Africa. The crater floor also holds lions, hippo pools, and elephant herds that never need to leave.

Destination Photography · Laikipia Plateau
Northern Kenya
Laikipia Plateau
Northern Kenya's private ranch conservancies on the Laikipia Plateau are some of the least visited, most diverse wildlife areas in East Africa. Wild dogs — rarely seen in the Mara or Serengeti — are resident here. Reticulated giraffe, Grevy's zebra (the world's most endangered zebra), and elephant move through private land where the camp guest count might be eight people total. Horseback safaris at dawn cover terrain in a way that no vehicle can approach, and the ranches operate with a conservation ethic that predates the tourism model entirely.

Destination Photography · Akagera · Rwanda
Rwanda
Rwanda & Akagera
Akagera National Park on Rwanda's eastern border completes an East Africa circuit that would otherwise miss savanna wildlife entirely. The park's conservation story — lions reintroduced in 2015, black rhino in 2017, both populations growing — is one of the most meaningful in the region. Combined with gorilla trekking in the Volcanoes National Park, Rwanda offers two entirely distinct wildlife experiences within a country the size of Maryland. The drive between them takes four hours; the contrast between the volcanic forest and the acacia savanna is complete.

When to Go

Kenya & Tanzania by Season

Peak Migration Window
July – October
The primary window for East Africa. Dry season across Kenya and Tanzania concentrates wildlife at water sources and keeps the bush open for sightings. The Mara River crossings — the most dramatic wildlife spectacle in the world — peak in this window, with timing varying annually. All ecosystems perform at their best: Amboseli, Ngorongoro, Laikipia, and the conservancies. Book camps six to nine months in advance. This is when we get the most demand and where our conservancy relationships matter most.
Best Overall Window
Green Season
January – March
The Serengeti calving season runs January through March on the southern plains — one of the most underrated wildlife events in East Africa. Eight thousand wildebeest calves born daily at peak, with predator activity that rivals the dry-season crossings. Lush landscape, dramatically lower camp rates, and the best birdwatching of the year. Not the right window for first-time travelers who want to maximize predator sightings across multiple ecosystems, but ideal for the Serengeti specifically. Ngorongoro Crater performs excellently year-round.
Shoulder Season
October – June
October and November offer Tarangire National Park in Tanzania at its best — elephant concentrations in the tens of thousands as the rains drive them to the Tarangire River, one of the most underused wildlife spectacles in East Africa. The long rains of April and May close most Mara camps and are not recommended. June is the transition into the dry season — wildlife is building but the crossings haven't yet begun, rates are slightly lower, and the Laikipia ranches are excellent. A good shoulder option for the right traveler.

Common Questions

Kenya & Tanzania — FAQ

Questions we hear most when planning East Africa travel — from the Kenya vs Tanzania decision to migration timing, conservancies, and how to combine both countries.

What's the difference between Kenya and Tanzania for safari?+
Kenya's primary advantage is the private conservancy model around the Masai Mara — exclusive access, no vehicle limits, night drives, and walking permitted. Tanzania's parks are larger, more iconic in scale, and include Ngorongoro and the southern Serengeti calving plains. Kenya tends toward intimacy and exclusivity; Tanzania toward grandeur and scope. A combined itinerary captures both, and for most serious travelers, it's the right answer.
What is the best month for Great Migration river crossings?+
July through October is the window, but the crossings don't follow a fixed calendar. In some years the first major crossings happen in late July; in others, not until mid-August. The northern Serengeti crossings typically precede the Mara crossings. We track this annually using real-time camp reports and position clients in the right camp at the right time rather than booking a calendar window and hoping the herds cooperate.
What is a private conservancy versus the national reserve?+
The Masai Mara National Reserve is a public park with open vehicle access — during peak migration season, it can have dozens of vehicles at a single crossing. The private conservancies bordering the reserve (Olare Motorogi, Mara North, Naboisho) are privately leased from Maasai landowners, charge a conservancy fee per night, limit total guests to roughly twenty-four, and permit night drives, off-road driving, and bush walks that the park prohibits. The wildlife moves freely between the two areas. The experience does not.
Is a combined Kenya and Tanzania itinerary worth the complexity?+
Yes, with twelve to fourteen days. The two countries have entirely different camp models, different terrain, and different strengths at different times of year. A well-sequenced combined itinerary covers more of what East Africa actually is than a single-country trip of the same length. The logistics — typically a charter flight between the Mara and Serengeti — add cost and one connection, but they're very manageable with proper planning.
What is the malaria situation in Kenya and Tanzania?+
Both Kenya and Tanzania are malaria zones, including the safari areas. Prophylaxis (typically atovaquone/proguanil or doxycycline) is strongly recommended. Most camps take significant precautions — treated nets, long-sleeve evening attire, spray at dusk — and serious malaria cases among safari guests are rare. Nairobi is at altitude and generally considered low-risk, but the Mara and Serengeti ecosystems are genuine malaria country. We provide a full health brief with every itinerary and recommend a travel clinic visit six to eight weeks before departure.
How far in advance should I book a Mara conservancy camp?+
For the peak July–October migration window, nine to twelve months in advance is the realistic answer for the best conservancy camps. Some camps open a specific dates window on a fixed calendar date and fill within days. Shorter lead times are possible in the shoulder months, and last-minute openings occasionally appear, but planning East Africa around availability rather than camp quality is a mistake we consistently advise against.
Amboseli versus Masai Mara for a first-time safari traveler?+
These are not competing options — they're complementary. Amboseli offers the best elephant viewing in Africa, a dramatically photogenic landscape with Kilimanjaro as backdrop, and a slower, more considered pace that suits travelers who want depth over volume. The Mara (especially the conservancies) offers the highest predator density and migration spectacle. A Kenya itinerary that combines both gives a first-timer the full range of what the country does best. Four nights Mara, three nights Amboseli is a common structure.
Is Tanzania without the Serengeti worth it — Selous, Ruaha?+
Yes, for the right traveler. Selous (now Nyerere National Park) is the largest game reserve in Africa by area and sees a fraction of the Serengeti's visitors. The Rufiji River boat safaris and walking safaris are among the finest in the country. Ruaha is Tanzania's best-kept secret — enormous lion prides, wild dogs, and an ecosystem that feels genuinely remote. Neither has the migration, but both offer wildlife experiences at a depth that the Serengeti's main circuit cannot match. We recommend them for travelers who have already done the Serengeti and want to go further into Tanzania.
