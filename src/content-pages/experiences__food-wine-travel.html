Why Food & Wine Travel

The Best Food Travel
Isn't About
Michelin Stars

A Michelin reservation is a transaction. What makes food travel genuinely memorable is different — it's the Parmigiano-Reggiano producer who lets you into the aging cave at 5 a.m. because you know someone who asked. The Burgundy négociant whose allocation list is closed to most of the world but who will taste you through twenty vintages if the introduction comes from the right place. The tsukemen shop in a Tokyo alley that hasn't changed its broth recipe in thirty-four years and seats nine people at a counter.

Great food travel is built around the chain from source to plate. It starts at the market before the restaurants arrive to shop. It goes to the producer before it goes to the chef. It eats at lunch counters and standing bars and family trattorias alongside the people who live there — and it uses the formal dinner as punctuation rather than the whole sentence. That's the version worth building a trip around.

The destinations that reward food travel most are the ones with the deepest culinary traditions — places where the food is an expression of the landscape, the season, and a way of life that hasn't been entirely absorbed by tourism. Italy's Emilia-Romagna. Burgundy. The Basque Country on both sides of the border. Kyoto. Lima. The places where what's on the plate has taken centuries to become what it is, and where understanding it changes how you see everything else.

Six Destinations With Centuries of Culinary Depth

The best food destinations are the ones where the cuisine is inseparable from the landscape, the season, and the people who make it. These are the places where that condition holds most completely.

Destination Photography · Emilia-Romagna Italy
Northern Italy
Italy
The country with the greatest density of genuinely excellent food traditions per square kilometer on earth. Emilia-Romagna is the center of gravity — Parmigiano-Reggiano, Prosciutto di Parma, aceto balsamico tradizionale, fresh egg pasta, mortadella. Each has a production story worth following to the source. Beyond the food valley: Piedmont for Barolo and white truffles in October and November, Sicily for olive oil and street food culture that has no equivalent, Campania for San Marzano tomatoes and the original Neapolitan pizza tradition. Italy is not one food destination. It is several dozen, each with a distinct culinary identity that took centuries to form.

Destination Photography · Japan
East Asia
Japan
The most food-serious culture on earth, organized around a concept — shun — that has no direct translation: the moment when an ingredient is at its absolute peak and nothing else is appropriate. Tokyo has more Michelin stars than any city in the world and also the best standing ramen counter, the best tsukiji tuna auction, and a sake brewery tradition that rewards the traveler who finds a small producer rather than a famous label. Kyoto for kaiseki — the most refined expression of seasonal Japanese cuisine — and Osaka for the opposite: takoyaki, okonomiyaki, and the city's proudly gluttonous street food identity.

Western Europe
France
Burgundy for the wine — Domaine visits in Gevrey-Chambertin and Meursault, a cellar dinner with a négociant, the Hospices de Beaune auction in November. Lyon for the bouchon tradition — the most honest expression of French cooking, unchanged by fashion. The Basque Country for pintxos bars, Idiazabal cheese, and txakoli wine in a culture that straddles the border with Spain and acknowledges neither country's full authority over its food. Périgord for black truffles and duck confit in January, when both are at their peak.

Southern Europe
Spain
San Sebastián has more Michelin stars per capita than any city in the world — and its most interesting food is in the pintxos bars of the old quarter, where the counterwork is changed twice daily and the best ones run out by 8 p.m. La Rioja and Ribera del Duero for wine country that rewards the visitor who gets off the tour bus route. Andalusia for Ibérico ham, fino sherry culture, and olive oil from estates that have been producing for a thousand years.

Destination Photography · California
North America
California Wine Country
Napa and Sonoma together represent one of the great food and wine regions in the world — the conversation stops being about domestic travel the moment you sit in a hillside cave in Rutherford with a winemaker who has been making the same Cabernet from the same block for thirty years. Harvest season in September and October is the window worth planning around. Combine with the Bay Area's restaurant culture — the farm-to-table tradition was invented here — for a complete food trip that never requires a passport.

Destination Photography · Peru
South America
Peru
Lima has produced the most exciting food culture in the world over the past twenty years — not through a single restaurant but through a movement that runs from Virgilio Martínez's Central to the cevicherías in Miraflores to the market stalls in Surquillo where the biodiversity of Peru's 4,000 varieties of potato is the daily reality rather than a talking point. The cuisine of the Andes, the Amazon, and the Pacific coast are three distinct food traditions operating within a single country — and the best Peruvian chefs treat all three as a living archive worth preserving.

What Every Food & Wine Trip Includes

The difference between a food trip and a remarkable food trip is almost entirely in the access layer — and access is built through relationships, not bookings.

⬡
Producer Access
Farms, cellars, aging caves, and production facilities that don't appear in travel guides and don't advertise to tourists. A Parmigiano producer at dawn. A Burgundy domaine during harvest. A sake brewery outside Tokyo by appointment. The source visits that make the restaurant visits mean something.
⬡
Market Guidance
Which markets, which stalls, what time to arrive, and what to buy. Every great food city has a market that the restaurants shop and a separate market that tourists visit. We send clients to the first one with a culinary guide who knows the difference between the vendor who has been there for forty years and the one who arrived last season.
⬡
Culinary Guides
Food specialists, not tour leaders. Trained cooks, food historians, and sommeliers who can explain what they're tasting and why it matters — not people who are enthusiastic about food in the way that everyone is enthusiastic about food. The guide who led your Emilia-Romagna day has written about Italian food culture. The Burgundy contact has worked three harvests at a premier cru domaine.
⬡
Restaurant Curation
The reservations worth having — and more importantly, the ones that aren't on any list but should be. The trattoria that doesn't take bookings except for regulars. The counter restaurant that releases tables on a Tuesday morning for the following month. The wine bar with a list that reflects twenty years of an owner's obsession. We handle the reservations that require knowing who to call and when.
When to Go

Food & Wine Travel by Season

Harvest & Truffle
September – November
The most concentrated food travel window of the year. Burgundy harvest runs late September through October — the one time of year that makes a domaine visit feel like a living event rather than a museum. Italy's white truffle season peaks October through December in Piedmont and Umbria. Napa and Sonoma crush in September and October. Bordeaux harvest in late September. If there is a single window worth organizing a food trip around, this is it.
Best Food Travel Window
Spring Markets
April – June
Spring produces the ingredients that serious cooks spend the rest of the year talking about. White asparagus in Alsace and the Veneto. Morel mushrooms in Burgundy and the French countryside. New season olive oil from southern Spain and Sicily available at the source. Artichokes in Rome and the Lazio coast. Sakura season in Japan coincides with bamboo shoots, spring clams, and the most anticipated ingredients of the Japanese culinary calendar.
Year-Round Destinations
Any Month
Japan and Peru are outstanding food destinations year-round — both organize their cuisine around seasonal availability, so every month produces a different menu worth traveling for. San Sebastián's pintxos culture runs twelve months. California wine country is accessible year-round, with harvest the peak window. For travelers with flexible schedules, shoulder season food travel frequently offers better producer access and smaller groups at the facilities worth visiting.
A Note from Mark

On Food & Wine Travel

Mark Sole
Founder & Travel Director · Hit Your Mark Travel

"The restaurant reservation is the last thing I build a food trip around — not because restaurants don't matter, but because the visit to the producer is what makes the restaurant meal mean something. When you've spent the morning in a Parmigiano aging cave and watched the cheesemaker tap each wheel with a small hammer to check for internal voids, the shaved Parmigiano at dinner that night is a different experience. The wine at a cellar dinner in Burgundy tastes different after you've spent the afternoon with the winemaker who made it and watched them walk the vineyard explaining what the frost did to the 2021 vintage. That chain from source to plate is the whole point of food travel. The restaurants are part of it, but they're never the beginning of it. The beginning is always the person who made what's on the table."
