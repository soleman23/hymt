Not the Map

Choose the kind of trip before the countries. For a first visit, one or two countries in 10–14 days works better than five. Food and wine trips are strongest in September and October; city and art trips work year-round. May–June and September–October balance weather and crowds better than July and August. Most trips here run 7–14 days.

Europe is easy to reach and easy to over-plan. The countries are close together, the trains are fast, and the temptation is to see all of it at once. The trips that work start from a clear answer to one question: what is this trip for — a first look at the classics, food and wine, art and history, the journey itself, or time together as a family?

A First Trip to Europe

Start with: Italy, or London paired with Paris.

Pace: 10–14 days, two or three bases. One country, or two connected by train — London and Paris are about 2 hours 15 minutes apart by Eurostar.

Best fit: Travelers who want the famous places done well, with private guiding at the sites that are hardest to visit independently.

Tradeoff: The classics are busy in every season. Fewer bases means leaving some well-known cities for a second trip.

Food and Wine

Start with: Italy, France, Spain or Portugal — one wine region with a city on either end.

Pace: 7–10 days, usually one country. A countryside base for four or five nights does more than a different region every two days.

Best fit: Travelers who would rather spend the afternoon at one estate than see three towns.

Tradeoff: Harvest weeks book early, and estate visits are by appointment. Wine country generally needs a driver.

Read next: Food and wine travel and the Mediterranean in October

Art, History and Culture

Start with: Rome and Florence, Athens and the Peloponnese, or Andalusia.

Pace: 8–12 days, with no more than one major site or museum each day.

Best fit: Travelers who want context from a specialist guide rather than a checklist of sights.

Tradeoff: Timed-entry tickets and guide availability set the schedule, so the dates need to be fixed early.

Read next: Culture and immersive travel

A Rail Journey

Start with: Switzerland’s Glacier Express, or a city route through France and Italy.

Pace: 7–10 days. Treat the scenic train as one full day, not a transfer.

Best fit: Travelers who like the journey itself and prefer not to drive.

Tradeoff: Luggage on trains is your problem unless a transfer is arranged, and scenic services sell out in peak months.

Read next: The Glacier Express, honestly

Family and Multigenerational

Start with: Italy, Portugal or the UK and Ireland, from a villa or one well-located hotel.

Pace: 7–12 days, one or two bases. Unpacking once matters more with children or grandparents.

Best fit: Groups that need connecting rooms, a pool, short travel days and something for every age.

Tradeoff: School holidays fall in peak season, when villas and family rooms go first.

Read next: Family travel and multigenerational trips

A Season-Led Trip

Start with: October for the Mediterranean and harvest; winter for cities, theatre and the Alps.

Pace: Depends on the season. Shorter city breaks work well in winter.

Best fit: Travelers with flexible dates who would rather match the place to the month.

Tradeoff: Late in the season, some ferries, island hotels and mountain services reduce or close. Confirm what is operating for your dates.

When to Go

Europe by Season

Shoulder Season
April – June & September – October
The best European travel window by a significant margin. Crowds at major sites are manageable. Temperatures across the Mediterranean, Tuscany, and Provence are ideal. September and October add harvest season in Burgundy, Tuscany, and the Douro Valley — the single best window for food and wine travel across the continent. Rates at top properties are meaningfully lower than peak summer.
Best Overall Window
Summer
July – August
The Mediterranean, Greek islands, and Scandinavian coasts are at their best for sailing and long evenings. July and August in Italy, France, and Spain bring crowds at iconic sites and significantly elevated hotel rates. The Greek islands and Croatian coast perform well in summer for crewed yacht charters and island-hopping. Scotland and Ireland are excellent in summer — warm, green, and considerably emptier than the south.
Winter
November – March
The Christmas markets of Germany, Austria, and Prague reward the cold. Paris in November is quieter than at any time in summer, and museums are easier to see well. London in winter is exceptional for theatre. The Canary Islands and Madeira offer milder Atlantic island weather through the winter. Ski season in the Alps from December through March.

Common Questions

Europe — FAQ

Questions about timing, itinerary depth, country combinations, and how to get more out of European travel than most people do.

May–June is ideal for most of Western Europe — warm, less crowded than July–August, and attractions fully open. September–October delivers excellent weather, harvest seasons in wine country, and noticeably fewer tourists. July and August are the most popular but also the most crowded and expensive. Winter is excellent for cities — Paris, Rome, and Vienna are best without summer crowds.

Two to three countries allows genuine depth. Four or more produces a rushed experience — too much transit, too little time in each place. There is a strong argument for spending two weeks in one country (Italy, France, Spain) rather than rushing through five. The best European trips go deeper rather than wider.

City-country combinations produce the richest trips. Rome and Tuscany. London and the Cotswolds. Paris and Burgundy or Provence. Barcelona and the Costa Brava. A week in a city followed by several days in rural surroundings suits most travelers better than exclusively one or the other.

Italy, France, Spain, Portugal, Greece, and the UK and Ireland are the six I plan most.
