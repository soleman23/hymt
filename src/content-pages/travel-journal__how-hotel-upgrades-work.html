Every few months, someone tells me about the email they sent a hotel before their anniversary trip — what they were celebrating, how long they had planned it. Sometimes a bottle of wine was waiting. Usually the room was the one they booked. A free upgrade is real, but it is not something a traveler can plan a trip around, and the people who are disappointed are almost always the ones who tried.

The short answer

A complimentary hotel upgrade is never guaranteed. Even at Marriott, where elite members with 50 or more qualifying nights a year are eligible for suites, the program rules make it “subject to availability upon arrival.” If a view, a suite or a second bedroom is the point of the trip, book and pay for that category. Treat anything better as a bonus.

"Book the room you would be happy in. If a specific room is essential, pay for it. The upgrade is the hotel's decision, made when you arrive."

Decide first: request it, or book it

Most upgrade disappointment starts with the wrong decision at booking, not the wrong request at check-in. Start from what the trip needs.

What actually affects a complimentary upgrade

Availability comes first. A hotel can only move you into a better room that is empty for your whole stay. Marriott's rules, for example, apply its top-tier upgrade “for the entire length of stay,” which means the better room has to be free every night you are there. That is why a long stay in a busy week is harder to upgrade than a short one, not easier.

Status in that hotel's own program. Loyalty status is the most clearly written upgrade benefit, but it is written narrowly. At Marriott, suites enter the picture only from Platinum Elite upward, and some brands and residence clubs are excluded. Status with one chain means nothing at an independent hotel, and many of the hotels my clients care about most are independent.

How the reservation is booked. Hotels see the channel and the rate. A booking made through an advisor network that has a program with that hotel arrives with the program's benefits attached, and upgrade consideration is often among them — again, based on availability. A rate from a third-party discount site may not qualify for loyalty benefits at all, so check the rate rules before you assume your status will apply.

Timing, a little. Arrival-day decisions depend on which rooms have been vacated and cleaned. A later arrival sometimes leaves more options than an early one, but this is a tendency, not a rule, and it is not worth rearranging a travel day for.

What you can request, and what is guaranteed

Guaranteed: the room category you booked and paid for, a paid upgrade the hotel confirms, and an upgrade confirmed with points or a program award before arrival. Marriott, for example, lets eligible members apply Nightly Upgrade Awards to a stay, and they must be used for every night of it.

Requestable, not guaranteed: a complimentary upgrade, early check-in, late check-out, a high or quiet floor, and bed type. Make these requests, and have them on the reservation before arrival, but do not build the trip on any of them.

If the hotel offers to sell you an upgrade at check-in, judge it as a purchase: is that specific room worth the nightly difference to you? Sometimes it is. A better category can be most of what you will remember about a beach stay.

When to ask

Ask early, through the booking. Preferences and occasions belong on the reservation itself, ideally when it is made and again shortly before arrival, so they reach the people preparing arrivals. At check-in, a polite question is fine — the answer depends on what is available that day, and it is not a negotiation. Mentioning reviews or social media followers does not help, and at a serious hotel it tends to work against you.

How advisor programs differ from loyalty status

Hit Your Mark Travel is part of the Travel Leaders Network. Its parent company, Internova Travel Group, runs a luxury hotel program called SELECT Hotels & Resorts. In its March 2, 2023 program announcement, the listed guest benefits for bookings made through affiliated advisors included a room upgrade “based on availability,” daily breakfast for two, a hotel amenity worth up to $100, and early check-in or late check-out when available.

Three qualifications matter. Only participating hotels are covered. Benefits can change, and the exact list depends on the property, rate and dates. And like loyalty status, the upgrade is consideration, not a confirmed room. When I quote a hotel, I tell you which program applies, what it lists for your stay, and whether the rate is eligible — before you pay.

My recommendation

Choose the room first and the upgrade strategy second. Decide which room feature would genuinely change the trip, pay for that category, and let everything else be upside. If you are comparing similar categories at one resort, what hotel room descriptions actually mean covers the wording that hides the difference, and the Maldives overwater versus beach villa comparison shows the same decision for one destination.

When you send me a trip brief, include the occasion, any must-have room feature, and loyalty memberships you hold. I will tell you which hotels on the shortlist have a program benefit for your dates, and which room is worth booking outright.

Where This Actually Moves the Needle

Occasions are worth telling a hotel about well ahead of arrival — romance and celebration travel covers how a honeymoon or anniversary is set up through the reservation rather than emailed in the week before.

On beach and island trips, the room category is the decision worth making deliberately, because the gap between two categories at the same property is most of the trip.
