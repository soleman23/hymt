The Country

Seychelles Is Not
a Maldives With Hills.

Seychelles works as a three-island week. Mahé, Praslin and La Digue sit 15 minutes to an hour apart by scheduled ferry, so you move between islands rather than staying put on one. April and October are the calmest months, between the two monsoon windows: the southeast trades blow May to September and push seaweed onto southeast-facing beaches, and the northwest monsoon runs November to March.

The question that actually gets asked is Seychelles or the Maldives, and they are not the same holiday. In the Maldives the resort is the island: you land, you transfer, and your map for the week is one lagoon and one reef. Seychelles is a country you move around inside — Mahé, Praslin and La Digue have towns, roads, bus routes and shops, and a scheduled catamaran runs between them on a published timetable. If you want nothing asked of you, the Maldives does it better and I will say so. If you want to be somewhere rather than on something, this is the one.

The three islands are not interchangeable and the order matters. Mahé is the arrival island, the largest, and the only one with a mountainous interior, a capital and a road network. Praslin holds the Vallée de Mai, the palm forest inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage site in 1983, where the coco de mer grows wild. La Digue is the small one, mostly cycled, and where the photograph everybody has seen was taken.

One structural thing before you compare hotels: outside a handful of private islands, Seychelles is not an all-inclusive country. It runs on hotels, guesthouses and licensed self-catering houses, and you go out to eat. That is a feature if you want a Creole restaurant scene and a Tuesday market, and a drawback if your idea of a beach holiday is a wristband and a buffet — in which case book the Maldives, or a resort island here.

Seychelles itineraries built around the ferry timetable, the direction each beach faces, and the difference between an island that needs three nights and one that needs two.

April or October · 9–10 Days
The Three-Island Week
The version of Seychelles that justifies the distance, and the one the ferry network exists for. Two nights on Mahé to start — the Victoria market on a weekday morning, a walk in the mountain forest, and a first swim on the southwest coast rather than the hotel strip. Catamaran to Praslin for three or four nights: the Vallée de Mai early, before the tour boats land, then Anse Lazio and Anse Georgette, and a day boat to Curieuse in between. A short crossing to La Digue for three nights — a bicycle instead of a car, Anse Source d'Argent at opening, and the east-coast bays almost nobody bothers with. Back to Mahé the day before you fly.

Safari First, Seychelles Second

What We Add to Seychelles Planning

Almost every decision that decides how this week feels is made before you book: which island, in which order, on which coast, and in which month.

⬡
Which Island, In Which Order
Most self-planned Seychelles trips get the order backwards. Mahé is treated as an airport with a hotel attached and given one night; La Digue is given six because of one beach. The version that works gives Mahé two nights at the front, so the first day is a mountain walk and a market rather than a transfer, then moves outward to Praslin and finishes on La Digue, where doing nothing is easiest. Every move costs a half-day. Three islands wants nine or ten days. Two islands is the honest answer to a week.
⬡
Which Way the Beach Faces
This is the single most useful thing anyone can tell you about a Seychelles hotel, and no booking site will. From May to September the southeast trades blow steadily, which cools the islands and also pushes seaweed onto southeast-facing bays and roughens the water there. From November to March the pattern reverses. A beautiful property on the wrong coast for your month means wind on the terrace and weed in the shallows for a week. The fix is not a better hotel, it is the same standard of hotel facing the other way, and it costs nothing to get right at the booking stage.
⬡
Ferries, Aircraft and the Half-Day You Lose
The scheduled catamarans between Mahé, Praslin and La Digue are the backbone of a trip here and they are also the part most easily got wrong. Sailings are limited, they fill in the high weeks, the crossing is less comfortable when the trades are up, and a booking made against the wrong sailing turns a beach day into a departure-lounge day. Light aircraft between Mahé and Praslin are the alternative and they change the arithmetic on arrival day, particularly with a late long-haul landing. We build the island order around published sailings and confirm them again before departure, because timetables here are seasonal.
⬡
Self-Catering Is a Real Category Here
Seychelles has a large, licensed self-catering and guesthouse sector that has no equivalent in the Maldives, and ignoring it removes the best-value half of the country from your search. A licensed house on Praslin with a car and a cook two evenings a week is a different trip from a resort room, and for families and longer stays it is frequently the better one. The trade is real: no room service, no concierge, and a standard that varies more than any rating suggests.
When to Go

Seychelles by Season

The Transitions
April & October
The two months between the monsoon windows, when the wind drops and the sea goes flat. Water clarity is at its best for diving and snorkelling, and neither coast is taking the weather — the only time of year beach orientation stops mattering. Four degrees south of the equator, Seychelles has no cold season and very little temperature range across the year; what changes is the wind, not the thermometer. Climate normals for Victoria are published through the World Meteorological Organization. These are also the shortest windows and the first to sell.
Best Overall Window
Southeast Trades
May – September
The drier, breezier half of the year, and the one that suits anybody who finds equatorial humidity hard work. The trades blow steadily from the southeast, which keeps the evenings pleasant and the mosquitoes down, and stacks seaweed on southeast-facing bays. Plan onto north- and west-facing beaches — the north of Mahé, the north and west of Praslin — and the season is a strength rather than a compromise. Ferry crossings are choppier in these months. This window also overlaps the dry safari season on the mainland, which is why the pairing works.
Northwest Monsoon
November – March
Hotter, more humid and wetter — though the rain mostly arrives as heavy afternoon bursts rather than as days lost indoors. The wind reverses to the northwest, so the calm, clean water moves to the southeast-facing coasts, and beaches that were weeded in July are the ones to be on in January. The inner islands sit close enough to the equator to be outside the main southwest Indian Ocean cyclone belt, unlike Mauritius and Réunion further south. It is the right window for the southeast coasts and a quieter island, and the wrong one if you came primarily to dive.

Common Questions

Seychelles — FAQ

The questions that come up most when planning a first Seychelles trip — how it compares to the Maldives, which months work, how the ferries run, and what the seaweed reports actually mean.

Seychelles if you want to move between islands; the Maldives if you want one resort island and nothing asked of you. Seychelles is a populated country with towns, buses, a market, a Creole kitchen and a scheduled ferry network, so a week here has geography in it. The Maldives is a set of one-island resorts where the property, the lagoon and the reef are the entire trip. Seychelles also has hills, forest and walking, which the Maldives does not, so if one half of the party wants an excursion and the other wants a sunbed, it handles that better. If everyone wants the sunbed, book the Maldives and skip the ferries.

April and October are the calmest months, sitting between the two monsoon windows. In those weeks the wind drops, the water clears and neither coast is taking weather — the only time of year when which way your beach faces stops deciding the holiday. The trade-off is availability: two months is a narrow target and the good properties on Praslin and La Digue commit early. May to September is drier and breezier, provided you are booked onto a north- or west-facing beach. November to March is hotter and wetter, with the calm water on the southeast coasts. There is no bad month here, only a wrong coast for the month you chose.

By scheduled catamaran ferry: roughly an hour from Mahé to Praslin, roughly fifteen minutes from Praslin to La Digue. The islands are connected by public transport on a published timetable, not by a seaplane the resort books for you. Sailings are limited in number, fill in the busy weeks, and are less comfortable when the southeast trades are up, so the crossings should be chosen before the hotels rather than after. Light aircraft also run between Mahé and Praslin and are worth the swap on a tight arrival day. Timetables are seasonal and change; check the current one with the inter-island ferry operator and confirm before booking.

It is the most common complaint in reviews of otherwise excellent hotels, and it is almost always a booking error rather than a hotel failure. Nine or ten nights for all three main islands; seven nights is enough for two of them. Every island move costs the better part of a day once you count packing, the crossing and checking in again. Six nights split three ways is the most common self-planned mistake here: two travel days out of six, and no morning anywhere at its best hour. If the trip has to be a week, Praslin and La Digue is the stronger pair, with Mahé kept for the arrival and departure days. If it can be ten nights, add Mahé at the front, where a mountain walk and the Victoria market beat a transfer.

Seychelles issues no tourist visa: visitors receive a permit on arrival, but an online travel authorisation must be obtained before you board. The permit conditions are the usual ones: a passport valid for the stay, confirmed accommodation, a return or onward ticket, and proof of funds. It is not a formality to leave until the week of departure, because approval is not instantaneous and airlines check it at the gate. Requirements and fees change, and the Seychelles government sets them, not the airline. Apply through the official Seychelles travel authorisation portal and verify the current rules before booking.
