The Island

Greenland Has No Road
Between Its Towns.

No road connects any two towns in Greenland, so every itinerary is a chain of flights, boats and weather. Plan 8 to 12 days for one region and build in at least one spare day before any international departure. June through September opens the ships and the hiking; February through April is the dog-sled window in Disko Bay and on the east coast.

The Greenland tourist board states the thing most itineraries quietly assume away: there is no road between any two towns. Nuuk, Ilulissat, Kangerlussuaq and Tasiilaq are joined by aircraft, helicopter and boat, and all three stop when the weather says so. That is not a footnote. It is the structure of the trip. An eight-day plan with eight moving parts and no spare day is not a plan — it is a bet on a fortnight of clear Arctic skies.

Ilulissat is the part that survives every version of the itinerary. Sermeq Kujalleq, the glacier feeding the Icefjord, moves about 19 metres a day and calves more than 35 cubic kilometres of ice a year — roughly a tenth of everything Greenland sheds, and more than any glacier outside Antarctica, which is why UNESCO inscribed the 402,400-hectare site in 2004. You hear it before you see it. The boardwalk to the fjord mouth is worth an evening; the boat is worth a morning.

After that the question is which coast, not how many. Disko Bay is the reachable one and takes most of the traffic. Tasiilaq and the east are harder, emptier, and hold the better sea ice. South Greenland is green in a way the rest of the country is not, with sheep farms and Norse ruins on fjords the ice never chokes. Trying all three inside twelve days means most of the trip is spent in small terminals. This is not a destination for anyone who needs the day to run to a schedule.

Disko Bay With the Slack Built In
Four nights in Ilulissat rather than two, because the Icefjord is a different place in flat evening light than in flat morning cloud and you want both chances. One full boat day among the bergs, one evening on the boardwalk, one day north to the Eqi calving front. Then the ferry or a short hop across to Qeqertarsuaq on Disko Island for two nights of basalt coast and whale water. Return through Kangerlussuaq or Nuuk with a night held at the gateway before the international sector — not as padding, but because a cancelled coastal flight otherwise costs the flight home. Ten days rather than eight buys the Eqi lodge night and a second attempt at the fjord in decent light.

Tasiilaq · East Greenland
March – April · 7–9 Days
The Sea-Ice Window at Tasiilaq
The east coast while the fjord ice still holds, which is a narrower window than most people plan for. In through Kulusuk, then across to Tasiilaq for the base. Two or three full days on a sled behind a working team, out onto the frozen surface of Sermilik Fjord and up into the valleys behind the town, with a hut night if the conditions and the driver agree. Long light, no engine noise, and a pace set by the dogs rather than by you. Days off the sled are for the town, the mountains and waiting out wind, which you will do at least once.

Scoresby Sund · Greenland
August – September · 11–13 Days
Scoresby Sund by Small Ship
The largest fjord system on earth, on a coast with one settlement in it, and no way to see it except from a hull. The ice clears late, which is why these sailings run at the back end of summer rather than the middle. Ittoqqortoormiit, a few hundred people on a shore with nothing either side of it, then days working the inner arms of the sound among tabular bergs that dwarf the ship. Most departures route through Iceland at one end. Ship choice decides this trip more than the map does: passenger count, ice class, and whether the expedition team is a team or a cruise director.

Kujataa · South Greenland
July – August · 8–10 Days
South Greenland on Foot and by Boat
The green end of the country, and the one region where boats replace flights for most of the moving. In through Narsarsuaq, then across the fjord to the Norse landing site at Qassiarsuk and up the valley to Igaliku, where the walking is genuinely good and the ruins are in fields rather than behind rope. Hvalsey church on the way to Qaqortoq, the hot spring at Uunartoq if the sea allows, and a day at the ice-cap edge. Slower, warmer and far less trafficked than Disko Bay, with the trade-off that there is no single sight here on the scale of the Icefjord.

Why Plan With Us

What We Add to Greenland Planning

Greenland punishes tight itineraries more reliably than any destination we sell. Most of the value here is in what the plan does when a sector does not fly.

⬡
Spare Days Are a Line Item
Fog at Kulusuk, wind at Ilulissat and low cloud in the fjords cancel Air Greenland sectors on a schedule nobody controls, and a missed coastal leg cascades into the international one behind it. So we write the buffer in rather than hoping. At least one spare night at the departure gateway, never a same-day connection onto a long-haul, and a written fallback for each leg: which boat, which later frequency, which town you sit in. Clients who have travelled with us in the Arctic mostly used the buffer once and were glad it existed.
⬡
The Airport Decides the Shape
Greenland's air map is being rebuilt, and it changes what a sensible itinerary looks like. Nuuk's 2,200-metre international runway opened in November 2024 under Kalaallit Airports, the first scheduled nonstop from North America began in June 2025, and Ilulissat and Qaqortoq are the next runways in the same programme. Kangerlussuaq was the single hub for decades and is no longer the automatic answer. Which gateway you fly into determines the direction of travel, the number of internal sectors and where the spare night sits, so we settle it before anything else.
⬡
Beds Are the Real Ceiling
The constraint in Greenland is not seats, it is rooms. Ilulissat's entire hotel inventory would fit inside one resort in most destinations, Tasiilaq runs on a handful of properties and guesthouses, and the good sled drivers and boat operators are individuals with a season's worth of days to sell. In practice that means the itinerary is built backwards from the scarce thing — the sled team, the Eqi lodge night, the cabin on the ship — and the flights are fitted to it. For summer in Disko Bay and the March window on the east coast, that conversation starts about a year out.
⬡
Which Ship, and On What Standards
A Greenland sailing is bought on four things, and the brochure leads with none of them: passenger count, ice class, the expedition team, and the operator's standing with AECO, the Arctic expedition cruise association whose guidelines govern landings, wildlife distance and community visits. Under about 150 passengers means more landings and fewer rotations waiting on deck. A team of naturalists and geologists is a different voyage from a cruise director with a microphone. On the east coast, where a fjord can close behind you, ice class stops being a specification and becomes the itinerary.
When to Go

Greenland by Season

Arctic Summer
June – September
Everything is open at once: ships along the west coast, hiking on tundra rather than snow, calving at its most active, and daylight that removes the deadline from the day. The sun does not set in Ilulissat from late May to late July, which changes how a boat day is planned — the best light on the bergs is often near midnight. Scoresby Sund and the east coast come later, from August, because the sea ice has to go out first. It is also when the small bed inventory is fully committed, so booking runs a year ahead.
Best Overall Window
Sea Ice & Sled
February – April
The specialist window, and the reason to come outside summer. Sled dogs are worked north of the Arctic Circle and on the east coast, and the season runs while the fjord ice holds — roughly February into April around Disko Bay and Tasiilaq, later in the far north. It ends when the ice breaks up, not on a date, so March is the safer booking than late April. Light returns quickly through this period, and the aurora is still available at the front of it. Cold, bright, and the Greenland least like anywhere else.
Dark Season
Late September – March
Aurora season, with real limits attached. The nights are long enough for northern lights from late September, and inland Kangerlussuaq has the driest, clearest air in the country for it. Against that: coastal flights thin out, many boat operators and some hotels close for the winter, and cancellations rise exactly when the schedule has fewest alternatives. Come in this window for the sky specifically and carry a longer buffer than summer needs, or come in March and take the aurora at the front of the sledding season.

Common Questions

Greenland — FAQ

The questions that come up most when planning a first Greenland trip — timing, how you actually move between towns, how much slack the itinerary needs, and whether to see it from a ship or a base.

June through September for ships, hiking and the calving front; February through April for dog sledding on sea ice. Those are two different trips, not two dates for the same one. Summer is when the west coast sailings run, the tundra is walkable and the light is endless — the sun does not set in Ilulissat from late May to late July. The east coast and Scoresby Sund open later in that window, from August, once the sea ice clears. The sledding window closes when the ice breaks up rather than on a calendar date, which is why March books more reliably than late April.

By air and by boat, with a helicopter or small fixed-wing aircraft covering most legs between settlements. Visit Greenland is explicit that no road links any two towns; the longest road in the country runs out of Kangerlussuaq to the ice-sheet edge and joins nothing. In summer, the coastal ferry works the west coast between the southern towns and Ilulissat and is a genuine alternative to flying if you have the days. In winter, snowmobile and sled cover short local distances. The practical consequence is that every transfer is a scheduled service with a weather condition attached to it.

Build at least one spare day before any international departure, and two if the trip includes the east coast. Fog at Kulusuk and wind in the fjords cancel Air Greenland sectors regularly, and the frequencies are thin enough that a missed leg is a lost day rather than a lost hour. Never book a same-day connection from a coastal flight onto a long-haul. The second consequence is softer but matters more: an itinerary with no slack turns every forecast into an argument, and the whole point of the place is that you are not in charge of the day.

Greenland sits outside both the Schengen Area and the European Union, so a Schengen visa does not cover it. Greenland is part of the Kingdom of Denmark and entry is administered through the Danish authorities, but visitors who need a visa need one specifically endorsed for Greenland. Many nationalities, including most North American and European passport holders, enter without one for short stays. Rules and endorsements change, so check the current requirement against the Danish Immigration Service rather than against what a previous traveller did, verified August 2026, and confirm before booking flights.
