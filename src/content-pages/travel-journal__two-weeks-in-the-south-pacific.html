Fourteen days sounds like plenty for a few islands. Then the travel days get counted: a long flight out, a long flight home, and every island change in between. The South Pacific rewards staying put, and the two-week trips that disappoint are almost always the ones that tried to see one more place.

The short answer

Two weeks in the South Pacific comfortably holds two island bases in one country, three at most. From the US West Coast, the flights out and home take two to three calendar days of a 14-day trip, and each island change uses most of another. Combining Fiji and French Polynesia is rarely worth it: in November 2026 we found no nonstop flight between them.

"Plan two weeks as two places, four nights or more in each. The third island is the one that turns a holiday into a schedule."

Count the days before the islands

Start with the days that are not on an island. This is the arithmetic for a 14-night trip from the US West Coast; it changes with your home airport and your flight times.

That is why the number of stops matters more than the list of islands. Each extra base takes a day of the holiday and adds a connection that can go wrong.

Three routes that fit 12–15 days

These are planning shapes, not priced itineraries. Each keeps to one country, which is the single biggest decision in a two-week South Pacific trip. Flight and boat schedules change by season, so every leg is confirmed for your dates before anything is booked.

French Polynesia: Tahiti, Moorea and Bora Bora

Shape: 13 nights. One night near Papeete if you land late, four or five nights on Moorea, six or seven on Bora Bora, and back to Papeete for the flight home.

Legs: Moorea is a ferry crossing from the Papeete terminal. Bora Bora is a domestic flight, which from Moorea may route via Papeete; from Tahiti it takes about 50 minutes, or up to an hour and a half with a stop, according to Tahiti Tourisme. The airport sits on a motu, and luxury hotels meet flights by boat.

Best fit: Couples and honeymoons who want one lively island and one lagoon stay. Families can do it with connecting rooms booked early.

Watch: Inter-island checked-bag allowances depend on the fare and can be lower on small aircraft, so check the allowance on the ticket. Keep a buffer between the return flight from Bora Bora and the international departure from Papeete.

Fiji: a Mamanuca or Yasawa island and a northern island

Shape: 13 nights. Six nights on an island in the Mamanuca or Yasawa groups, five on Taveuni or Vanua Levu, and a final night near Nadi if your flight home leaves early.

Legs: Resort ferries run daily from Port Denarau to the Mamanuca and Yasawa islands, and water taxis and seaplanes are faster alternatives, as Tourism Fiji describes. Fiji Link and Northern Air fly from Nadi to the smaller airports, including those for the northern islands.

Best fit: Families, and travelers who want a beach week followed by a greener, quieter island for diving or walking.

Watch: The date line costs a calendar day on the way out. Small aircraft and seaplanes have tighter baggage limits than international flights; pack for the smallest plane on the trip.

Cook Islands: Rarotonga and Aitutaki

Shape: 12 nights. Five on Rarotonga, six on Aitutaki, and a last night on Rarotonga before the flight home.

Legs: Rarotonga to Aitutaki is a domestic flight of under an hour; see Cook Islands Tourism for international and domestic options. International access from North America is more limited than to Tahiti or Fiji, so the route usually depends on which connection runs on your dates.

Best fit: Travelers who want a smaller, simpler trip: one island with a town and culture, one with a lagoon.

Watch: The international schedule sets the shape of this trip. Confirm it before choosing hotels.

Two bases or three?

Three bases can work in two weeks, but only when all of these hold: every move is short, ideally a ferry or a single flight under an hour; no move falls on the first or last day; and the party genuinely prefers variety to rest. Tahiti, Moorea and Bora Bora come closest: a ferry, then a domestic flight that sometimes routes back through Papeete. A Fiji route that adds a northern island after a Yasawa stay usually does not, because it runs back through Nadi.

If any of those fail, take the third island out and give its nights to the second base. Nobody returns from the South Pacific wishing they had spent a fifth day in an airport.

The do-less version

One island, one resort, ten to twelve nights. It is the option most travelers rule out first and many would enjoy most. You unpack once, there is no connection to miss, and the days that would have gone to transfers go to the lagoon. It suits a honeymoon, a family with young children, or anyone who is tired before the trip begins. If you want variety, change rooms rather than islands: a few nights in a beach villa and a few overwater, at the same resort, is a move of a hundred yards.

If you have ten days, or three weeks

Ten days leaves about seven or eight full days on the ground. That is one base, or two only when the connection between them is short, such as Tahiti and Moorea. It is the trip the do-less version was made for.

Three weeks makes a third base comfortable and a second country possible. Choose a second country only where the international connection is direct or the stopover is somewhere you would happily spend a few nights; otherwise the extra week is better spent going deeper in one place, such as a remote atoll or a second island group.

Mistakes that overpack a two-week trip

  • Two countries. Fiji, French Polynesia and the Cook Islands are hours apart by air and not always directly connected. In November 2026 we found no scheduled nonstop between Nadi and Papeete; connections commonly ran through Auckland. That is a day each way spent on a second country.
  • Four bases. Three nights per island sounds efficient. It means a travel day every third day.
  • Same-day connections to the international flight. An outer-island flight or boat that runs late should not cost you the flight home.
  • Forgetting the date line. Count nights by the calendar at your destination, not by the days you are away.
  • Packing for the long-haul allowance. The smallest aircraft on the route sets the luggage limit.
  • Assuming every island connects directly. Many routes go back through a hub. Map the actual legs before choosing the islands.

When to go

Fiji and French Polynesia both have a generally drier season from May to October, according to Tourism Fiji and Tahiti Tourisme. It is also when island flights and the best rooms book first, so a dry-season trip needs earlier planning than a wet-season one.

My recommendation

Choose the country first, by your home airport and what the trip is for, then hold it to two bases with at least four nights each. Add a third only if you would rather see more than rest, and keep the last connection well clear of the flight home.
