The Lagoon Really Is
That Color.

The overwater bungalow as a hotel format was invented in French Polynesia in 1967 by three California hoteliers who realized that Moorea's lagoon depth was perfect for stilted construction. The format has now been replicated across the Maldives, the Caribbean, Fiji, and the rest of the South Pacific — but the original lagoon, the one in the photographs that justified the trip, remains here. Bora Bora's lagoon is the one. The color is genuinely that color in person; the photograph doesn't exaggerate. The property selection is what determines whether you get the lagoon at its best or a less remarkable corner of it.

Moorea is the underused alternative to Bora Bora — a more dramatic island silhouette (the volcanic ridge runs sharper, with peaks visible across the lagoon from every property), about half the cost of equivalent Bora Bora accommodation, and a more authentic island feel because Moorea retains a meaningful local population beyond the resort workforce. Many of our most experienced South Pacific clients now skip Bora Bora entirely on return visits in favor of Moorea or the Tuamotus, treating Bora Bora as a one-time pilgrimage rather than the destination they choose to return to.

The Tuamotu atolls — Rangiroa and Fakarava in particular — are a different French Polynesia entirely. Diving instead of lagoon swimming, the south pass of Fakarava with its 700-shark wall, the inside-the-pass current dive at Tiputa on Rangiroa where the marine life concentrates against the incoming tide. Far less developed than the Society Islands (Tahiti, Moorea, Bora Bora), with smaller pensions and lodges instead of resort properties, and an entirely different version of French Polynesia that adds significantly to a multi-island trip.

What We Add to French Polynesia Planning

French Polynesia is the destination where the property selection on Bora Bora, the island sequencing through Air Tahiti, and the right combination of Society Islands and Tuamotus produce the trip that justifies the distance.

⬡
Resort & Villa Selection on Bora Bora
Bora Bora has approximately a dozen resort properties on the motu islets surrounding the main island. The lagoon view, the villa category, the position of the property relative to Mount Otemanu, and the consistency of the service vary significantly across them. The difference between the right villa at the right resort and a mid-tier choice at a similarly priced property is significant — visible immediately on arrival. We know which villa at which property delivers the experience that the rates are intended to buy.
⬡
Air Tahiti Routing
The Air Tahiti inter-island network is the only way to combine multiple French Polynesian islands, and the routing matters. Some islands have only morning or evening flights, some routes only operate two or three days a week, and the order in which islands are sequenced affects the total trip length and the airport-to-resort transfer logistics. The most efficient routing typically begins with the closer Society Islands (Moorea), then moves to Bora Bora, then to the Tuamotus if included, then returns through Papeete.
⬡
Island Combination Logic
Bora Bora alone for less than 7 days is the most common mistake travelers make. The honeymoon-only Bora Bora trip works for travelers who want exactly that and accept the trade-off, but adding 3–4 nights of Moorea, Huahine, or a Tuamotu atoll dramatically increases the geographic and experiential variety of the trip. We help structure the right combination based on what each traveler is actually looking for, rather than defaulting to the single-island Bora Bora package.
⬡
Whale Season Timing (Moorea)
Humpback whales migrate to Moorea's waters from August through October to calve in the warm lagoons. The encounters — by certified operator, snorkel-only, on a small boat with a marine biologist guide — are among the great wildlife experiences available in the South Pacific. For travelers whose trip can be timed in August–October, adding the whale day to a Moorea stay changes the texture of the trip entirely. The timing is narrow and the operators with the right credentials are limited; we book this in advance.
When to Go

French Polynesia by Season

Dry Season
May – October
The reliable window for French Polynesia. Cooler air temperatures (in the upper 70s to low 80s), drier weather, lower humidity, and the calmest lagoon conditions of the year. The trade winds from the southeast keep the air moving and the temperatures comfortable. Lagoon water clarity is at its peak, making this the best window for diving in Fakarava and Rangiroa. Whale season runs August through October in Moorea. Peak tourist months are July and August (US/European summer holidays), which fill the Bora Bora resorts and warrant booking 6–9 months ahead. May, June, and September are the sweet spot — same weather, lower crowds, more accessible rates.
Best Overall Window
Whale Watching
August – October
Humpback whales migrate from Antarctica to French Polynesian waters to mate and calve in the warm lagoons. Moorea is the primary destination for whale watching — certified operators run small-boat trips with snorkeling permitted under specific protocols (no chasing, no aggressive approach, swim-pause-observe). The experience of being in the water at close range with a humpback mother and calf is among the great wildlife encounters in the South Pacific. The season is narrow and the operators with the right credentials are limited; travelers planning specifically for whale season should book 6+ months ahead.
Wet Season
November – April
The wetter, warmer season — air temperatures in the mid-80s with higher humidity, more frequent (but typically short) rain showers, and occasional tropical depressions. The water remains warm and the diving conditions are still good, but visibility on the open ocean dive sites is slightly reduced. Rates at the Bora Bora and Moorea resorts drop meaningfully outside of the December–January holiday peak. For travelers willing to accept some weather variability, January–March is a viable window with significantly lower resort rates. February is the rainiest month and the one we typically recommend avoiding.

Common Questions

French Polynesia — FAQ

Questions we hear most when planning French Polynesia — from Bora Bora property selection to Moorea vs Bora Bora, Tuamotu diving access, and how many islands to combine.

Bora Bora for the iconic lagoon and the destination-archetype trip — the postcard view, the overwater villa over Mount Otemanu, the property quality at the top end. Moorea for a more dramatic island silhouette, a more authentic Polynesian feel (Bora Bora's economy is almost entirely resort-driven; Moorea retains a meaningful local population), half the price of equivalent Bora Bora accommodation, and the unique addition of whale watching August through October. Many of our most experienced South Pacific clients now skip Bora Bora on return visits in favor of Moorea. For a first French Polynesia trip, combining both for 4–5 nights each is the strongest single answer.

On Bora Bora and Moorea: yes, for honeymoon or special-occasion travel. The experience of waking up over the lagoon with reef fish visible through the glass floor and direct ladder access to the water is genuinely distinct from a beach bungalow. The premium varies by resort — typically 30–60% more than equivalent beach villas. On the Tuamotus, where the focus is diving, the overwater format is less essential and the savings on a lagoon-edge bungalow can be redirected toward dive packages.

For a first trip: two islands (typically Moorea and Bora Bora) for 8–10 days minimum, ideally 10–12 days. Single-island Bora Bora trips of fewer than 7 days are the most common mistake — the island is small and the activities are concentrated, so anything more than 5 nights begins to feel slow without adding a second destination. For 12+ days, a third island (a Tuamotu atoll for divers, Huahine for the authentic alternative, Tetiaroa for the Brando) significantly expands the trip. The Air Tahiti routing accommodates multi-island moves efficiently if planned with the right sequence.

May through October is the dry season and the right answer for almost every traveler. June and September are the sweet spot — same weather as the July–August peak, with lower crowds and better rates. August through October adds the Moorea whale watching season. November through April is the wet season — typically still warm and the rain is usually brief, but with more weather variability and higher risk of tropical storms; significantly lower resort rates outside the December–January holiday peak.

Open Water minimum, Advanced Open Water strongly recommended. The Tuamotu pass dives (south pass of Fakarava with the shark wall, Tiputa Pass on Rangiroa) involve current and depth conditions that benefit from the Advanced certification. Snorkeling alone in the Tuamotus is excellent at the inside-lagoon coral gardens and L'Aquarium-type sites, but the genuinely extraordinary experiences (the 700-shark wall, the dolphin encounter) require diving. Discover Scuba programs are available for first-time divers and provide a meaningful first experience without full certification.

Direct flights to Papeete (PPT) operate from Los Angeles (8 hours, daily), Honolulu (5.5 hours), Paris (22 hours via West Africa), Tokyo (10 hours), Auckland (5 hours), and a handful of other gateways. From the US, the LA–Papeete route on Air Tahiti Nui, French Bee, or United is the standard. The arrival is typically late evening; a Papeete overnight on the night of arrival is usually the right structure rather than connecting immediately to Bora Bora. Departures are similarly late evening, allowing a final-day return to Papeete from the islands without a rushed connection.

Variable. The overwater villa format is generally restricted by minimum-age policies at the top resorts (often 12+, occasionally 7+). Beach villas at the same resorts are family-friendly without age restrictions. Moorea is the most family-suitable major island — abundant snorkeling, whale watching, the pineapple plantation, and resort properties with active kids' programs. Bora Bora is more honeymoon-focused at most of its resorts. Outside the resort circuit, the islands are not heavily oriented toward family travel infrastructure. We match families to properties that explicitly accommodate the age range of the traveling children.

French Polynesia has more dramatic island topography (the volcanic peaks of Mount Otemanu and Mou'aroa visible from the lagoons), generally larger and more architecturally varied resorts, the Polynesian cultural overlay, and the option to add the Tuamotu diving or the Marquesas cruise for a multi-region trip. The Maldives has the most consistently extraordinary lagoon water clarity in the world, smaller and more intimate resorts (often the entire resort occupies a single small island), better-priced overwater villa options at the mid-tier, and an Asia-side travel access from Europe and the Middle East. For US travelers, French Polynesia is the natural choice; for European and Middle Eastern travelers, the Maldives has the geographic advantage.
