The Country

India Rewards Preparation
More Than Any Country.

The window for most of India is October through March, when the north is dry and cool enough to walk a fort at midday. A first trip runs 12–16 days: the Golden Triangle of Delhi, Agra and Jaipur takes 7–8 of them, and the rest goes to Rajasthan, Kerala or a tiger park. The Taj Mahal closes every Friday.

India is not a country you improvise. The distances are long, the internal flights matter, the difference between a good guide and a licensed one is the difference between a monument and the argument the monument was making, and the gap between a heritage hotel that is genuinely a family seat and one that borrowed the word is invisible from a booking site. Every one of those is a planning decision made months ahead, and each of them compounds.

The Golden Triangle earns its reputation and also collects the most rushed itineraries in Asia. Delhi, Agra and Jaipur can be crossed in four days, and are, constantly. Seven or eight days across the same three cities is a different trip: the Taj Mahal at opening rather than at ten, a morning in Old Delhi with someone who can read the Mughal and colonial layers off the street plan, an afternoon in a Jaipur block-printing workshop where the blocks are cut by hand and the indigo vat is live. The itinerary looks similar on paper. It is not the same country.

South India is a separate decision, not an extension. Kerala runs on a different calendar, a different cuisine and a different pace — the backwaters near Alleppey, the tea country above Munnar, and an Ayurvedic tradition where the serious centres want two weeks and a consultation, not a spa afternoon. Pairing north and south in twelve days produces two hurried halves. Pairing them in sixteen produces the trip most people were imagining.

Kerala · India
November – March · 9–11 Days
Kerala and the South
South India as a first trip rather than an add-on, and a gentler introduction to the country than the north for travellers who want one. Three nights Fort Cochin for the colonial layers and the food, a single night on the backwaters — one is the right number — then four nights in the hills above Munnar on a working tea estate, with walking, plantation mornings and a genuine Ayurvedic consultation rather than a spa treatment. Finish with two nights on the coast north of Kochi. Shorter driving days than Rajasthan, a milder climate window, and the cuisine that most surprises people who thought they knew Indian food.

What We Add to India Planning

India is the destination where the difference between a well-planned trip and a poorly planned one is largest, and where it shows up on the first morning rather than the fourth day.

⬡
Guides Who Read Buildings
A licensed guide at the Taj Mahal will give you the dates, the marble inlay technique and the story about the hands of the craftsmen. A Mughal architecture specialist will show you why the building sits off-centre in its own garden, what the char bagh layout is arguing about paradise, and how Humayun's Tomb in Delhi is the draft that this is the final version of. India's monuments were built to be read, and most visitors are handed the wrong reading list. Getting this right is the single highest-leverage decision in the itinerary.
⬡
Pace and Internal Flights
The most common failure in an India itinerary is not a bad hotel — it is a day lost to a drive that should have been a flight, repeated four times. Delhi to Agra is a reasonable road day. Agra to Jaipur is a reasonable road day with Fatehpur Sikri in the middle. Almost everything else across the north and to the south wants an internal flight, and the timing of those flights determines whether a morning is a monument at opening or a check-in queue. We build the ground around the air schedule rather than the other way round.
⬡
Heritage Property Selection
"Heritage hotel" in India covers a family seat still occupied by the family, a genuinely restored fort under professional management, and a 1990s build with crenellations added to the roofline. The first two are among the best stays in Asia. The third is a hotel with a costume. The distinction is invisible in photographs and largely invisible in reviews, because guests are comparing against expectations rather than against the category. It takes direct contact and honest reports from clients who have stayed, which is the part that does not scale and is the reason to use an advisor here.
⬡
The First Forty-Eight Hours
India rewards a soft landing. Most long-haul arrivals land in Delhi late at night, and the itineraries that fail are the ones that put a full Old Delhi walking day against jet lag on the first morning. A hotel near the arrival side of the city, a first day built around one thing rather than four, and a driver and guide who have been briefed that day one is a settling day rather than a schedule — that sequence decides how the following two weeks feel. It costs nothing and it is skipped constantly.
When to Go

India by Season

Cool Season
October – March
The window for the north, and the reason India's calendar is less flexible than most destinations'. Delhi, Agra and Rajasthan are dry and walkable, the forts are bearable at midday, and the light is long. December and January bring genuine cold to the north and morning fog that can delay flights and trains out of Delhi — worth building slack around rather than avoiding. Kerala and the south are also at their best in this window. This is peak season for a reason: the heritage properties in Rajasthan book months ahead, and the good guides are committed earlier than that.
Best Overall Window
Hot Season
April – June
The north becomes genuinely difficult — Rajasthan regularly passes 40°C and sightseeing compresses into early mornings and evenings. There is one strong reason to accept it: this is the best tiger window. As the seasonal water dries down, sightings at Ranthambore and the central Indian parks concentrate around the remaining waterholes, and rates across the north fall from their peak. For a wildlife-led trip with early starts and afternoons indoors, the trade is defensible. For a first trip built on forts and cities, it is not.
Monsoon
July – September
The southwest monsoon arrives in Kerala first, usually in early June, and moves north. Most tiger parks including Ranthambore close for the season and reopen from October. It is the wrong window for the Golden Triangle and for a first trip. It is the right one for two specific things: Kerala's Ayurvedic tradition treats the monsoon as the optimal season for treatment, and the Western Ghats are green rather than dust-coloured. Rates fall accordingly. Plan it deliberately or not at all.

Seven or eight, not four. Four is the standard packaged version and it works as transport between three monuments. Seven or eight gives Delhi three nights instead of one, Agra two so the Taj Mahal can be seen at opening rather than mid-morning, and Jaipur three with a day held in reserve. The distances have not changed; what changes is whether each city gets a morning at its best hour and an unscheduled day. In India the unscheduled day is reliably the one people describe afterwards.

Friday. The monument is closed to tourists every Friday for prayers at the mosque within the complex, and it is the single most common scheduling error in a self-planned India itinerary — arriving in Agra on a Thursday evening for a Friday morning visit. It opens around sunrise on the other six days, which is the hour worth taking: the marble reads differently in low light, and the forecourt fills through the morning. Ticketing and opening hours are published by the Archaeological Survey of India; confirm before booking, as timings around religious holidays vary.
