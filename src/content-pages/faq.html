Working With Us

We charge a planning fee that covers the time and expertise involved in designing your itinerary, researching and vetting properties, and managing the booking process from start to finish. The fee varies based on the complexity and length of the trip, and it's always discussed and agreed upon before any work begins.

In addition to planning fees, we also receive commissions from many hotels, resorts, and tour operators — which is standard practice in the travel advisor industry. These commissions never influence what we recommend. If a property pays us nothing but is genuinely the right fit for your trip, that's what we'll book.

All fees are disclosed transparently before you commit to anything.

Online booking tools are efficient for straightforward trips where you already know exactly what you want and the stakes are low. For anything more complex — multi-destination itineraries, high-investment trips, unfamiliar regions, or travel where a bad decision costs real money — they fall short in a few specific ways.

First, access. We book properties, rooms, and experiences that aren't available through standard consumer channels — preferred partnerships, suite upgrades, resort credits, and amenities that the same room costs more to access online. Second, knowledge. Knowing that a hotel photographs well is different from knowing which rooms face the wrong direction, or that the pool is under renovation in the season you're traveling. Third, advocacy. If something goes wrong, you're handling it alone at midnight from a foreign country. We're handling it for you.

The clients who benefit most from working with us are the ones who've had a trip go sideways on a self-booked vacation and decided they'd never do that again on a significant trip.

It starts with a conversation — either by phone, video call, or email — where we get into the specifics: where you want to go, what kind of experience you're after, how you travel, your budget, and any constraints or preferences we need to design around. This isn't a form you fill out. It's a real conversation.

From there, we build an itinerary proposal with specific properties, a day-by-day flow, and pricing. We refine it together until it's exactly right. Once you approve the itinerary and authorize payment, we handle all bookings, confirmations, and logistics. Before departure, we send a complete trip document with every confirmation, contact number, transfer detail, and anything else you need. During the trip, we're available if anything comes up. After, we debrief to understand what worked and what didn't — which improves future trips.

Booking & Payment

Payment typically happens in two stages. First, a deposit is required at the time of booking — the amount varies by supplier and will be clearly communicated before you commit to anything. Second, final payment is due closer to departure, again on a timeline determined by each supplier (typically 60–90 days out for most international trips, though this varies).

Planning fees, if applicable, are charged separately from supplier deposits and are generally due at the start of the planning process.

We accept all major credit cards — Visa, Mastercard, and American Express. Using a credit card for travel purchases is always recommended, as it provides an additional layer of purchase protection. Some suppliers may have preferred payment methods, which we'll communicate in advance.

Hotel and land prices are generally protected once a deposit is paid. Airfare is the exception — prices are not locked until ticketing is complete. We'll always advise you on the best timing to ticket flights based on the route and your itinerary.

Occasionally, a supplier will update their rates between quote and booking, especially if availability changes. We always confirm current pricing before collecting any payment and notify you of any changes before proceeding.

For most itineraries, we recommend letting us coordinate flights alongside the rest of your trip so that transfers, timing, and connections are planned properly. On some trips — particularly those using frequent flyer miles or with complex routing you've already mapped out — it makes sense for clients to handle their own air. We'll discuss what approach makes sense for your specific trip.

Section 03
Cancellation & Changes

Cancellation policies vary by supplier — hotels, airlines, cruise lines, and tour operators all have their own terms, and they're communicated clearly before any booking is made. Some reservations have penalty-free cancellation windows. Others are non-refundable from deposit. We make sure you understand what you're committing to before you commit.

To cancel, contact us in writing at mark@hymtravel.com. We process the cancellation with each supplier according to their terms and confirm the outcome to you in writing. Planning fees are non-refundable once planning has begun, regardless of whether a booking is made.

This is the biggest reason we strongly recommend travel insurance. Cancellation protection means that if you have to cancel for a covered reason — illness, injury, family emergency — your investment is protected. Without it, you absorb the full financial loss.

It depends on the timing and what's been booked. Early in the planning process, changes are usually straightforward. After deposits are paid, changes may involve cancellation penalties, availability constraints, or re-booking fees from suppliers. We'll always lay out exactly what a change will cost and what alternatives exist before proceeding.

Contact us as early as possible when you know a change is needed — earlier notice almost always means more options and lower costs.

This is one of the clearest advantages of working with an advisor rather than booking alone. If a supplier cancels, changes, or fails to deliver, we're the ones on the phone handling it — not you. We have direct supplier relationships and know how to escalate quickly.

What we can recover depends on the supplier's policies, the reason for cancellation, and whether you have travel insurance. We'll advocate hard on your behalf, but our ability to compel a supplier to act is limited by the terms of our agency relationship. Travel insurance fills the gap when a supplier can't or won't make you whole.

Force majeure events — hurricanes, earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, political instability, pandemics — fall outside standard supplier cancellation policies. Each supplier handles these situations differently. Some offer full refunds or rebooking. Others issue credits. Some release nothing unless you have travel insurance that covers the event.

We monitor conditions in your destination as departure approaches and will reach out proactively if something warrants attention. Our job during a disruption is to help you navigate options and recover as much value as possible. Travel insurance with cancel-for-any-reason or trip interruption coverage is the most reliable protection against force majeure losses.

Trip Logistics

We provide general guidance on visa and entry requirements for your destinations and flag anything that needs attention. We're not visa agents and don't process applications on your behalf, but we'll make sure you know what's required, what the timelines are, and where to go for the official application.

Entry requirements change. We always recommend verifying requirements directly with the relevant embassy or consulate — or with the official government source — in the weeks leading up to your trip. For passports, most international destinations require at least six months of validity beyond your travel dates. If yours is close, renew it before booking.

Yes. We communicate dietary restrictions and food allergies to all properties and operators at the time of booking and follow up closer to departure. Most quality hotels and lodges are well-equipped to accommodate common dietary needs — vegetarian, vegan, gluten-free, nut allergies, kosher, halal, and others.

For severe allergies, we go further — we identify properties with strong kitchen communication protocols and brief you on what to expect in each destination. Some destinations handle dietary restrictions more reliably than others, and we'll give you an honest picture of what to expect wherever you're going.

Yes. Accessibility varies enormously by destination and property, and we'll research the specific needs of your group against what's actually available — not just what a hotel's website claims. We verify room configurations, bathroom setups, elevator access, activity compatibility, and transportation options for travelers with mobility limitations, hearing or vision impairments, or other needs.

Some destinations and experiences are more accessible than others, and we'll give you an honest picture of what's realistic before you commit to an itinerary. Tell us exactly what you need and we'll design around it.

Yes. For most trips, we coordinate all transfers — airport to hotel, between properties, and any ground transportation needed throughout the itinerary. Having transfers arranged in advance removes one of the most common points of friction in international travel, especially on arrival after a long flight.

For self-drive itineraries — road trips through New Zealand's South Island, a drive through Morocco, or a self-guided tour of Portugal — we handle car rental arrangements and provide detailed routing and logistics.

We provide general destination health guidance — which destinations require proof of yellow fever vaccination, where malaria prophylaxis is typically recommended, and what travelers commonly report about health conditions in specific regions. We flag what to discuss with a healthcare provider.

We are not medical professionals and don't provide medical advice. For specific vaccination recommendations and any prescriptions you might need (malaria prophylaxis, altitude medication, etc.), consult a travel medicine clinic or your physician well before departure — ideally 6 to 8 weeks out, since some vaccinations require time to take effect.

We provide packing and gear guidance tailored to each specific itinerary — because what you need for a Botswana safari in the Okavango is very different from what you need for a Patagonia trekking trip or a week in the Maldives. Generic packing lists are close to useless. Destination- and activity-specific guidance is actually helpful.

Before departure, your trip documents include a packing section with specific recommendations — including what you genuinely need and what you'll never use.

Section 06
Travel Insurance

Yes. We recommend it on every trip, and we mean it. The question isn't whether something can go wrong — it's what the financial exposure looks like when it does. On a $20,000 international trip with non-refundable deposits, a medical emergency, or a last-minute family crisis, the difference between having insurance and not having it is potentially a five-figure loss.

Medical evacuation alone — being airlifted from a remote safari camp or a mountain region to a facility capable of treating you — can cost $50,000 to $200,000 without insurance. Your domestic health plan almost certainly doesn't cover international medical evacuation.

Declining insurance is your right. But if you're booking through us, we want you to make that decision consciously — not because it seemed like an easy place to cut costs.

At minimum, look for coverage in these areas:

  • Trip cancellation and interruption — reimburses non-refundable costs if you cancel for a covered reason (illness, death in the family, etc.)
  • Emergency medical expenses — covers treatment abroad, since most U.S. health plans have limited or no international coverage
  • Emergency medical evacuation — covers the cost of getting you to appropriate medical care, which can be extremely expensive in remote destinations
  • Baggage loss and delay — covers lost, stolen, or delayed luggage and essential replacement items
  • Travel delay — covers meals, accommodation, and rebooking costs for significant travel delays

If you want maximum flexibility, look for a policy with a Cancel for Any Reason (CFAR) rider. It's more expensive but covers cancellations that don't meet standard policy criteria. Note that CFAR typically needs to be purchased within 10–21 days of your initial deposit — so don't wait.

We are not licensed insurance agents and cannot advise on the suitability of any specific policy for your situation. Read your policy carefully and contact the insurer directly with coverage questions.

As soon as you make your first deposit. Two important coverages — pre-existing condition waivers and Cancel for Any Reason — typically have strict purchase windows that begin at your first deposit date. If you wait until your final payment to buy insurance, you may have already lost eligibility for both.

We'll remind you about insurance timing at the point of first deposit, but ultimately the responsibility to purchase is yours.

Some premium travel credit cards include meaningful travel benefits — trip delay coverage, baggage protection, and in some cases trip cancellation coverage. A few offer emergency medical benefits. Before relying on credit card coverage, read the actual benefit guide carefully and understand the coverage limits, exclusions, and claim process.

In most cases, credit card travel benefits are secondary coverage and have significantly lower limits than a dedicated travel insurance policy. For high-value trips or any destination where medical evacuation is a realistic risk, a standalone policy is a more reliable safety net.

On-Trip Support

Yes. One of the core reasons to work with an advisor rather than booking alone is that you're not handling disruptions by yourself. If a flight is cancelled, a hotel has a problem, a transfer doesn't show up, or anything else goes sideways, you contact us directly and we work the problem.

Your trip documents include our direct contact information. We ask that you reach out to us first — we can often resolve issues faster through supplier relationships than a client can through a standard guest services line.

Before your departure, we send a complete trip document that includes:

  • Full day-by-day itinerary with hotel names, addresses, and check-in/check-out times
  • All booking confirmation numbers
  • Transfer details — driver names, meeting points, pickup times
  • Activity and tour confirmations with operator contact information
  • Emergency contacts — our direct line, local destination contacts, and your travel insurer's emergency line
  • Destination-specific notes — currency, tipping guidance, cultural considerations, health precautions, what to expect on arrival
  • Packing guidance specific to your itinerary

We send this as both a digital document you can access offline and in whatever format is most useful to you. You should never be digging through your email trying to find a confirmation number when you're standing at a hotel front desk.

Contact us immediately — don't wait until you get home. The sooner we know, the more options we have. If a room is genuinely not what was promised, we'll work with the property to upgrade or move you. If an experience falls short, we'll explore alternatives.

When you return, we want to hear the full debrief — what worked, what didn't, what surprised you. That feedback directly improves how we plan trips in the same region going forward, and it holds suppliers accountable.

Contact us and contact your travel insurance provider's emergency line immediately — your policy documents will have a 24/7 emergency number. Your insurer's assistance line can help coordinate medical care, arrange evacuation if necessary, and manage the logistics of the situation. That's what the coverage is for.

Your trip documents will include the contact information for your insurer's emergency line alongside our direct contact. For non-urgent issues, the properties we book are accustomed to assisting guests with medical needs and can recommend local doctors or clinics.

Yes. After every trip, we do a debrief — usually a short call or email exchange where we go through what worked, what could have been better, and what surprised you. That feedback improves the next trip, and it helps us hold suppliers accountable when something falls short.

We keep notes on every client trip, so when you come back to plan your next one — whether that's six months later or three years later — we're starting from a real understanding of how you travel and what delivers for you.
