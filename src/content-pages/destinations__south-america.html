The Region

A Continent That Keeps
Surprising People

The W Trek in Patagonia — five days through the Torres del Paine national park, the granite towers at dawn, the Grey Glacier at its face, the Valle del Francés in the light of an afternoon that doesn't end until ten — is one of the most celebrated multi-day walks in the world. The lodge-supported version makes it accessible to travelers who want the landscape without the camping: a comfortable bed, a hot dinner, and a packed lunch prepared by someone who knows what you'll need at 1,400 meters.

The Inca Trail is equally demanding and equally transformative — but the two acclimatization nights in Cusco before the trek are non-negotiable, a planning detail that separates a good Machu Picchu experience from a miserable one. Lima has become one of the finest food cities in the world in the past fifteen years; the Central restaurant's tasting menu is built around altitude-zoned Peruvian produce that has no equivalent anywhere else. The Surquillo market is the food source. The culinary story connects.

Buenos Aires has a food and wine scene that would be internationally recognized if it were in Europe, a tango culture that is still a living tradition rather than a tourist performance, and a bookshop called El Ateneo Grand Splendid that happens to be inside one of the most beautiful theaters in South America. Colombia's Cartagena, Medellín, and the coffee region have been reborn as genuine travel destinations. South America is a continent worth planning properly.

Southern Cone
Patagonia
The Torres del Paine national park in Chilean Patagonia is one of the most extraordinarily beautiful landscapes accessible by any traveler willing to make the journey to the southern tip of the Americas. The W Trek — five days through the park's signature landscapes, from the Grey Glacier face to the mirador above the granite towers — is available in lodge-supported versions that provide a comfortable bed and dinner at the end of each day's walk without reducing the grandeur of what's in front of you. Argentina's Los Glaciares national park and the Perito Moreno glacier for travelers who want the ice without the trek. El Chaltén for serious trekkers and Cerro Torre. Best October through April in the Southern Hemisphere summer — book lodges in Torres del Paine ten to twelve months in advance for December and January.

Galápagos & Ecuador
The Galápagos Islands are the only place in the world where wildlife has evolved without predators and therefore has no fear of humans — a marine iguana that lets you step over it, a blue-footed booby that displays six feet away, a giant tortoise that has been walking the same volcanic path for a hundred years and sees no reason to change course for a visitor. A small-ship expedition liveaboard covers more islands, more ecosystems, and more wildlife than any land-based tour. Ecuador's cloud forest in the Andes for hummingbirds and orchids. Quito for the finest colonial architecture in South America and the best jumping-off point for the islands.

When to Go

South America by Season

Patagonia Summer
October – April
The primary window for Patagonia — Southern Hemisphere summer means long days (up to eighteen hours in December), stable weather windows in the notoriously changeable Torres del Paine, and wildflowers across the pampas. December through February is peak season — lodges book out and the W Trek is at full permit capacity. October/November and March/April offer the same conditions with lower occupancy. The Galápagos is excellent year-round; January through June offers calmer seas and warmer water temperatures ideal for snorkeling.
Best Patagonia Window
Andes & Peru Dry Season
May – October
The optimal window for the Inca Trail, Machu Picchu, the Sacred Valley, and high-altitude Andean travel — dry skies, clear mountain views, and the full range of agricultural terraces in use. June through August is the peak window for Peru; Inca Trail permits for this period sell out by March. May and September/October are excellent shoulder months with lower occupancy and the same conditions. The Pantanal wildlife viewing in Brazil peaks in July through October as water levels drop and animals concentrate around remaining water sources.
Year-Round Destinations
Buenos Aires · Colombia · Galápagos
Buenos Aires and the Argentine wine country are excellent year-round, with summer (December through March) being ideal for outdoor activity and winter (June through August) for cultural focus and lower rates. Colombia is consistent year-round — December through March and July through August are the driest periods. The Galápagos receives visitors year-round and each season has specific wildlife highlights: sea turtle hatching (December through March), Galápagos hawk breeding (June through December), and penguin activity (June through September).

Common Questions

South America — FAQ

Questions about Machu Picchu, Patagonia, country combinations, timing, and how to travel South America without the usual planning mistakes.

What are the must-see destinations in South America?+
Machu Picchu and the Sacred Valley in Peru is one of the world's iconic travel experiences. Patagonia — shared between Chile and Argentina — delivers some of the most dramatic landscapes on earth. The Galapagos Islands in Ecuador are extraordinary for wildlife and unlike any other destination. Buenos Aires is one of South America's great cities for food, architecture, and culture.
How long should a South America trip be?+
A focused Peru trip — Lima, Cusco, Sacred Valley, Machu Picchu — works in 10–12 days. A combination of Peru and Argentina requires 16–21 days for genuine depth. Patagonia alone fills 10–12 days if you're going deep into both the Chilean and Argentine sides. The continent rewards longer travel more than any aggressive multi-country itinerary.
Is Patagonia accessible to non-hikers?+
Much of it. The iconic landscapes of Torres del Paine and Argentine Patagonia can be experienced via day hikes, horseback riding, boat excursions, and vehicle-based drives without the multi-day trekking circuits. Several excellent lodges provide full access to the scenery without demanding exertion. The W Trek and Circuit are available for serious trekkers but are not required to experience Patagonia.
What's the best way to experience Machu Picchu without crowds?+
Arriving on the first bus (which requires staying in Aguas Calientes the night before, not day-tripping from Cusco) gets you into the site before the main crowds. Daily visitor numbers are capped and popular entry times sell out months ahead. I book access passes in advance and can also arrange Inca Trail permits for the full trekking approach if that is what you want.
Can I combine multiple South American countries in one trip?+
Yes, though internal flight distances are significant. Peru and Argentina are a popular pairing — Lima to Buenos Aires is around 4 hours. Adding Chile (Santiago and Patagonia) extends the trip with manageable logistics. Bolivia adds a dramatically different landscape layer — the Salar de Uyuni is among the most otherworldly environments in the world. The more countries you add, the more transit days you spend.
What is the best time of year for South America?+
Peru is best May–October (dry season) — comfortable temperatures and clear skies for mountain views. Patagonia peaks November–March (Southern Hemisphere summer) when Torres del Paine is fully accessible; most lodges and trails close outside this window. Buenos Aires is pleasant year-round, peaking in spring (September–November) and fall (March–May) for the most comfortable weather.
What is altitude sickness and how do I manage it in Peru?+
Cusco sits at 3,400m (11,200 feet) and most travelers experience some degree of altitude adjustment — headache, fatigue, and shortness of breath are common in the first 24–48 hours. Arriving in Cusco a day before any major activity, staying hydrated, and using altitude medication if recommended by your doctor are the standard mitigations. The Sacred Valley (2,800m) is a lower-altitude acclimatization option before Cusco.
Is a Galapagos cruise or a land-based trip better?+
A live-aboard cruise covers more islands, eliminates daily commuting by boat, and provides maximum time with wildlife — the standard recommendation for most visitors. Land-based trips from Santa Cruz are viable and more budget-friendly, with day trips to different islands. The best live-aboard small ships (under 20 passengers) provide naturalist guiding and itinerary flexibility that land-based options cannot match.
