The Region

The Camp Is Everything.
The Guide Is More.

The Great Migration peaks July–October, but Africa runs on six separate calendars rather than one. Botswana's dry season concentrates game June–October; Rwanda's gorilla trekking works year-round and depends on trail conditions instead. Most first safaris run 10–12 days across two or three camps. Camp selection matters more than country — it decides the guiding, the vehicle, and how many other vehicles share a sighting.

There are over a thousand safari camps and lodges operating across sub-Saharan Africa. The range in quality between the best and the rest is one of the largest in any travel category anywhere in the world. A top-ten Botswana camp and a mid-tier Tanzania camp cost within a few hundred dollars of each other per night. The experience they deliver is not in the same category.

The guide is the variable that matters most. A guide who has worked the same ecosystem for fifteen years reads animal behavior in ways that a less experienced guide doesn't — predicting where the leopard will move before it moves, positioning the vehicle for the light before the light arrives, staying at a sighting for the right amount of time instead of moving on when the drama pauses. That difference is only available at camps that hire and retain the right people, which is a short list that takes years to develop.

Africa also has more to offer than the safari circuit. Morocco is one of the great cultural travel destinations in the world. South Africa combines world-class wine country, penguin colonies, the Cape Peninsula, and private game reserves with malaria-free Big Five access. Rwanda's gorilla treks are among the most profound wildlife experiences on earth. The Seychelles sits off the same coast and answers a different question entirely — granite islands you move between on a scheduled ferry, not a single-resort atoll. We know all of it — and we know which version of each destination is worth the flight.

Camp & Lodge Selection

We know it — built from operator relationships and client feedback across every major ecosystem. The difference between a camp on that list and one that isn't is frequently invisible in the brochure and immediately apparent on the ground.

Guide Quality
The best guide on a mediocre camp delivers a better safari than a mediocre guide on the best camp. We know which properties retain their top guides year over year, which camps have naturalists with specialist qualifications in specific ecosystems, and which operations have the guide-to-guest ratios that allow for the kind of personalized wildlife reading that makes a safari genuinely exceptional.
⬡
Timing & Migration Windows
The Great Migration is a continuous cycle — wildebeest in the Serengeti southern plains for calving in January and February, moving north through June and July, crossing into the Mara between July and October, returning south by November. Being in the right place in the right week requires understanding exactly where in that cycle the herds are. We track this annually and build itineraries around it rather than marketing a window and hoping the timing aligns.
⬡
Combination Itineraries
Africa's best trips are rarely single-country. Kenya and Tanzania together. South Africa's safari combined with the Cape. Rwanda's gorillas followed by the Mara. Zambia's walking safaris paired with Victoria Falls. We build these combination itineraries with the same rigor we apply to individual countries — ensuring that the logistics, the pacing, and the camp selection across multiple countries produce a coherent trip rather than a collection of disconnected experiences.
When to Go

Africa by Season

Great Migration Peak
July – October
The primary East Africa window — dry season in Kenya and Tanzania, peak wildlife viewing across the Mara and Serengeti, and the wildebeest river crossings at their most dramatic in July through September. Also the best window for Botswana (dry season concentrates animals at waterholes) and Zambia's South Luangwa. South Africa's Sabi Sands and Kruger perform excellently year-round but peak for leopard sightings in the dry winter months of June through August.
Best Overall Window
Green Season
November – March
The wet season in East and Southern Africa — lush landscapes, dramatically lower rates (30–50% at many camps), newborn wildlife in January and February, and the best bird-watching of the year. The Serengeti calving season from January through March is an extraordinary wildlife spectacle in its own right. Morocco and North Africa are ideal in winter — cool, dry, and uncrowded. Rwanda's gorilla trekking is excellent year-round. Not recommended for first-time safari travelers who want the maximum predator viewing.
Shoulder & Transition
April – June
April and May bring the long rains to East Africa — most camps close or operate at reduced capacity, and this window is generally not recommended for Kenya and Tanzania. However, the shoulder months of late April and May represent excellent value for South Africa, where the dry season is just beginning. June is the start of the peak window in Botswana and Zambia. Victoria Falls is at its most dramatic in March through May when the Zambezi is in full flood.

Safety is destination and context-specific, not continental. The safari countries — South Africa, Kenya, Tanzania, Botswana, Rwanda — have well-established tourism infrastructure, professional operators, and dedicated wildlife areas with consistent security. It depends on the country and region. South Africa generally requires no vaccinations for most travelers. Kenya, Tanzania, and Botswana recommend or require malaria prophylaxis; yellow fever documentation may be required depending on transit countries. I provide a full health and entry brief with every itinerary and recommend consulting a travel health clinic 6–8 weeks before departure.

East Africa (Kenya, Tanzania) is defined by the Great Migration, open savanna landscapes, and Maasai culture alongside the wildlife. Southern Africa (Botswana, Zimbabwe, South Africa, Zambia) offers deeper wilderness, more intimate camps with fewer vehicles at any sighting, and higher concentrations of certain species like wild dogs and large elephant herds.

The dry season (May–October in most of Southern and East Africa) concentrates wildlife around water sources and keeps bush roads accessible. The Great Migration's dramatic river crossings peak July–October in Kenya and Tanzania. Gorilla trekking in Rwanda is possible year-round. The green season (November–April) offers lower prices, fewer visitors, and exceptional birdwatching.

At the luxury end, camps in Botswana start around $700–$1,500 per person per night all-inclusive. A meaningful Africa itinerary of 8–12 days typically runs $10,000–$25,000+ per person depending on destination, timing, and camp selection. South Africa delivers a stronger quality-to-cost ratio. Budget expectations vary significantly by country and style of travel.
