The Continent

The ship is the trip.

Everything about an Antarctic voyage follows from the vessel. A 500-passenger ship will show you the continent from the rail. A 100-passenger expedition ship will land you on it twice a day, because landing regulations cap the number of people ashore at any one time. The difference in price is real; the difference in experience is larger.

The second decision is the Drake Passage. Two days of open ocean each way, sometimes benign and sometimes genuinely rough, or a two-hour flight across it to King George Island. Flying saves four days and a great deal of uncertainty. Sailing is part of the pilgrimage and is how you see the albatrosses. Neither is wrong, but travellers rarely realise it is a choice until we raise it.

What We Add to Antarctic Planning

This is the most expensive trip most people ever take and the one with the least useful information available online.

⬡
Ship Selection
Passenger count, ice class, the ratio of guides to guests, and whether the expedition team is genuinely expert or seasonal crew. These determine how many times you get ashore. We have the operator relationships and the honest assessments.
⬡
Cabin Strategy
On a two-week voyage the cabin matters more than on any other trip, and the price range within a single ship is enormous. There are specific cabins on specific vessels that are worth the upgrade and others that are not.
⬡
Timing for Wildlife
Late October brings pristine ice and courtship. December and January bring chicks and the longest days. February and March bring the whales, in numbers that late-season travellers talk about for years. The month decides what you see.

The Drake Decision
Sailing or flying is the question that most affects how the trip feels, and the one most travellers have not considered. We work through it properly, including the contingency if the flight is weathered out.
When to Go

The Antarctic Season

The season runs roughly November to March. Each part of it is a different continent.

Peak Wildlife
December – January
The warmest weeks, close to twenty-four-hour daylight, penguin chicks hatching and colonies at their busiest. The most popular window and the one to book earliest.
Best Window
Early Season
November
Pristine, unbroken ice and courtship displays, with the landscape at its most photogenic before the season's traffic. Colder, and some landing sites are still inaccessible.
Whale Season
February – March
The best whale watching of the year by a distance — humpbacks and minkes feeding in the channels — with penguin chicks fledging and lower prices. Our most frequent recommendation for a second trip.

Common Questions

Antarctica — FAQ

Questions we hear most when planning Antarctica trips. More detail on the full FAQ page.

How far ahead do we need to book?+
Twelve to eighteen months for the best ships and cabins, particularly for December and January. Late availability exists but rarely on the vessels worth taking.
Do we have to cross the Drake Passage?+
No. You can fly across it in two hours from Punta Arenas and join the ship on the Peninsula. It saves four days. Sailing gives you the seabirds and the sense of arrival, and modern stabilised ships handle it far better than the reputation suggests.
When should we go for whales?+
February and March. Humpbacks and minkes feed in the channels late in the season and the encounters are extraordinary. Earlier in the season you will see far fewer.
Is it physically demanding?+
Less than people expect. Zodiac transfers require reasonable mobility and the landings involve walking on uneven ground, but there is no requirement to be especially fit. Operators differ in how they accommodate limited mobility and we match accordingly.
What does it actually cost?+
A Peninsula voyage on a good small expedition ship typically starts around fifteen thousand dollars per person and rises considerably with cabin category. South Georgia itineraries are substantially more. It is the most expensive trip most clients take.
Can we combine it with Patagonia?+
Yes, and most people should. Ushuaia sits at the end of Argentine Patagonia, so adding Torres del Paine or the Argentine lakes before or after the voyage is straightforward and makes far better use of the flights.
