Ancient History.
Genuine Hospitality. Wrong Assumptions.

The Middle East runs October through April; May to September is too hot for anything outdoors. Most itineraries run 8–12 days, and the region combines well — Jordan with Oman, or the UAE as a stopover on either end. Ramadan shifts roughly eleven days earlier each year and changes restaurant and site hours throughout, so confirm the dates before fixing travel.

Most travelers who have never been to the Middle East are operating from an image of the region that doesn't match the experience of arriving. Jordan is one of the safest and most welcoming countries in the world to travel — a Bedouin host in Wadi Rum who offers tea and stays for three hours because conversation is hospitality, a family in Petra who has been selling hand-painted ceramics from the same stall for three generations and knows the canyon better than any guidebook. Israel's Old City of Jerusalem contains more layers of human civilization per square meter than any other site on earth. Oman is the kind of country that travelers who have been tell every other traveler about — a road trip along the frankincense trail, a morning with a potter in Nizwa, a night in a remote desert camp beneath the Milky Way.

Egypt is one of the oldest continuous civilizations in the world and deserves more than a Pyramids afternoon and a souvenir market. The Valley of the Kings at Luxor with an Egyptologist who can read the hieroglyphics. A felucca sail on the Nile at dusk between Luxor and Aswan. The Abu Simbel temples at dawn, before the tour buses arrive, in a silence that makes the scale of the construction comprehensible. These are not difficult experiences to access — they require a guide, a plan, and knowing which morning to arrive.

When to Go

Middle East by Season

Primary Window
October – April
The optimal window for all Middle East travel — cool temperatures across Jordan, Israel, Oman, and Egypt; clear skies; and the full range of outdoor and archaeological site experiences available without heat management. October and November offer shoulder pricing with excellent conditions. February and March bring wildflowers to the Jordanian desert and the Israeli hillsides. April is Ramadan in some years — transformative for the right traveler, requiring extra planning for everyone.
Best Overall Window
Summer
May – September
Summer temperatures across the Middle East are extreme — Jordan and Israel reach 38–42°C in July and August, Egypt's desert regions are genuinely dangerous without proper preparation, and Oman's interior is best avoided entirely. The UAE and Gulf coast function in summer because of total air-conditioning infrastructure — Dubai in summer is an indoor experience, and perfectly manageable for travelers who understand this. The Red Sea coast of Jordan (Aqaba) and Egypt (Hurghada, Sharm el-Sheikh) are excellent for diving year-round, with summer water temperatures ideal for extended time in the water.
Ramadan Consideration
Variable · Check annually
Ramadan follows the Islamic lunar calendar, moving approximately eleven days earlier each year. Traveling in the region during Ramadan requires specific preparation — many restaurants are closed during daylight hours, the pace of daily life changes, and in some cities the atmosphere transforms entirely after sunset in ways that are genuinely extraordinary. Ramadan is not a reason to avoid travel; it is a reason to prepare differently. We advise every client on the Ramadan calendar as part of trip planning and help them decide whether the timing works for their specific itinerary.

Common Questions

Middle East — FAQ

Questions about safety, visas, dress codes, and what makes Jordan, UAE, and Saudi Arabia worth adding to your travel list.

The destinations I plan — Jordan, UAE, Saudi Arabia, Oman, and Qatar — are stable, well-established tourism destinations with professional hospitality infrastructure. "The Middle East" covers a vast region; the countries I recommend have strong records for traveler safety and a genuine interest in international visitors. I don't plan travel to areas where the security context doesn't support tourism.

Jordan alone — Amman, Petra, Wadi Rum, the Dead Sea — makes an excellent 7–10 day stand-alone trip. Dubai or Abu Dhabi work well as a destination or a stopover. Jordan and Israel together offer extraordinary historical depth across two culturally distinct but geographically close countries. Saudi Arabia's AlUla is a compelling emerging destination for archaeology and desert landscapes.

Jordan, UAE, and Saudi Arabia offer visa on arrival or e-visa options for US and most Western passport holders. Israel operates its own entry system. Evidence of a visit to Israel may complicate entry to certain Arab countries in some circumstances — this is evolving and depends on the specific passport. I verify current entry requirements for each destination and flag any potential complications in advance.

UAE and Jordan are relatively moderate — covered shoulders and knees are appropriate outside resorts and tourist areas, though international hotels are more relaxed. Saudi Arabia has specific public dress requirements for non-Muslims outside major tourist zones. Oman is similarly conservative beyond resort settings. I provide a practical cultural brief for every destination that covers local norms clearly.

Petra is one of the genuinely irreplaceable travel experiences. The approach through the Siq — a 1.2km narrow gorge carved into rose-red sandstone — leads to the sudden reveal of the Treasury, which is unlike anything else in the world. A single day is manageable; two days allows exploration of the Monastery, the High Place of Sacrifice, and the less-visited areas. Best at opening time before the crowds arrive.

Yes — this is a compelling combination for travelers who want to see the region broadly. Jordan and UAE are easy to combine (Amman to Dubai is a 3-hour direct flight). Adding AlUla in Saudi Arabia brings a dramatic desert archaeology component. A 12–16 day itinerary covers all three comfortably. Israel can be added to a Jordan trip if entry considerations permit.

October through April is the optimal window for most of the region — Jordan, Israel, Oman — when temperatures are comfortable for outdoor exploration (15–25°C). UAE and Qatar are best November–March when the heat is manageable. Summer (June–August) is extremely hot throughout the region and best avoided for outdoor-heavy itineraries, though desert camps and indoor experiences remain excellent year-round.

Wadi Rum is Jordan's vast desert valley. Sweeping red sandstone and granite, Bedouin culture, and one of the most dramatic night-sky environments in the Middle East. Overnight camps range from basic to highly designed tented glamping operations with en-suite bathrooms, good food, and guided 4WD excursions during the day. It is one of the most distinctive overnight experiences in the region and is worth at least one night on a Jordan itinerary.
