Why Formula 1

How to Plan a Formula 1
Weekend Worth Flying For

A Grand Prix runs Friday to Sunday, and the 2027 Formula 1 calendar lists 24 of them. There are four ways to watch: the Paddock Club, a grandstand, a yacht in Monaco's harbour, or a balcony above the track. The best of each commits 6–18 months out, and the seat, not the hotel, is the largest line on the trip.

A race weekend is three days of track time: practice on Friday, final practice and qualifying on Saturday, the race on Sunday, as Formula 1's own guide to the weekend sets out. Monaco, Monza and Abu Dhabi are Sprint weekends in 2027, which adds a short Sprint race on Saturday, so every day carries a session that counts.

At most destinations the hotel sets the tone and the price. At a Grand Prix the seat does both: it decides what you see, who you share it with, whether lunch is included, and how you leave a circuit that tens of thousands of others are leaving at the same moment. The rooms follow from it.

This page covers Monaco, Monza and Singapore, with Abu Dhabi as the season's last word. For tennis, golf and horse racing, start with sports and event travel.

Before You Commit

Who a Grand Prix Trip Suits, and Who It Does Not

A race weekend is loud, crowded and fixed to the minute. That is the appeal for some travellers and the problem for others.

⬡
Right for: the fan
Someone who follows the season and wants the moments television flattens: the start, the speed through a corner, the noise of a home crowd.
⬡
Right for: the occasion
A milestone birthday, a client weekend or a gift, where the setting matters as much as the racing: Monaco's harbour, Singapore's skyline at night.
⬡
Not right for: whole-lap watchers
No seat at any circuit shows the full lap, and on Monaco's street circuit any one seat sees a short stretch. If following every move of the race matters most, television does that job better.
⬡
Not right for: the noise- or crowd-averse
Even hospitality guests queue at the gates. Anyone who dreads crowds, or a Sunday-only budget at Monaco, is better served by another trip.

Four Circuits, Race by Race

A harbour, a park and a city at night. Dates are from the 2027 calendar published by Formula 1, checked 3 October 2026.

4–6 June 2027 · Monte Carlo, Monaco
Monaco Grand Prix
The race the others are measured against: 78 laps of a 3.337-kilometre street circuit, per the 2026 Monaco timetable, run between the Casino, the Fairmont Hairpin and Port Hercule. The race moved from late May to the first weekend of June in 2026, and 2027 is Monaco's first Sprint weekend.

All four ways to watch exist here, and nowhere else offers the last two. The Automobile Club de Monaco organises the race and sells the grandstands, with half-price tickets for ages six to fifteen and 10% off a two- or three-day package. Port Hercule has about 760 berths, and the ACM, the port authority and the Maritime Police set who may moor and board during the race. Private balconies and terraces above the circuit are also let for the weekend.

Visitors base themselves in Monaco itself or along the coast in Beaulieu, Cap-Ferrat or Nice, and come in by car, train or tender each morning. The weekend pairs well with a longer stay in France before or after.

3–5 September 2027 · Monza, Italy
Italian Grand Prix, Monza
The Temple of Speed opened on 3 September 1922, the third permanent circuit in the world after Brooklands and Indianapolis, according to the Autodromo Nazionale Monza. It sits inside the walled Parco di Monza, north of Milan, and it was built for speed: long straights, few slow corners.

The reason to go is the tifosi. When the chequered flag falls the gates open and the crowd floods the main straight to stand beneath the podium, a tradition Formula 1's fan guide to Monza describes. The circuit has confirmed a Sprint race for 2027.

Visitors typically base themselves in Milan or on Lake Como. Early September is one of Italy's two best travel windows, which makes race week the start of a longer trip through Italy rather than the whole of it.

8–10 October 2027 · Marina Bay, Singapore
Singapore Grand Prix
Formula 1's first night race, held on 28 September 2008 according to Formula 1. The 2026 race ran 62 laps of the 4.927-kilometre Marina Bay Street Circuit and started at 20:00 local time, per the 2026 Singapore timetable.

Sessions run late, so the trip works best on late hours: sleep in, eat late, keep the day free. The Singapore Grand Prix sells by zone, and the zone decides which grandstands, viewing platforms and stages a ticket reaches. Plan for heat and humidity even after dark.

The Tiers

Four Ways to Watch a Grand Prix

Each one sees something the others miss. None of them sees everything.

⬡
Paddock Club
Formula 1's own hospitality. The official Paddock Club puts guests in suites above the team garages, with pit lane walks and dining through the day. It sees the pit lane and the start. It does not see most of the lap.
⬡
Grandstand
A reserved seat from the official ticketing: the ACM at Monaco, Formula 1's own ticketing at Monza, the Singapore Grand Prix by zone. It sees one corner or straight up close, with the crowd around you. It carries no lunch and no lounge.
⬡
Yacht in Port Hercule
Monaco only. Boats moored on the trackside quays sit metres from the circuit, and the port's Grand Prix berth rates applied from the Monday before the race to the Monday after in 2025. It sees the harbour section. It does not see the climb or the Casino.
⬡
Balcony or Terrace
Monaco only: private terraces and balconies above the circuit are sold for the race weekend.

What It Costs

What a Formula 1 Weekend Actually Costs

Priced by the seat, not by the night
The seat sets the number: Paddock Club, grandstand, yacht and balcony are priced per race, per day and per guest, and the four tiers sit too far apart for one nightly figure to mean anything.
What actually sets the number
  • Which tier — a grandstand seat, the Paddock Club, a yacht berth and a private balcony are four different products, not four grades of one
  • Party composition — Monaco prices ages six to fifteen at half the adult ticket for Friday, Saturday or Sunday (ACM terms, checked October 2026)
  • Duration — a two- or three-day Monaco package takes 10% off the adult tickets; a single Sunday does not, and the discount excludes children's tickets

Common Questions

Formula 1 Travel — FAQ

Questions about booking a Grand Prix weekend: when to commit, which way to watch, children's tickets, where to stay and what happens if a race is called off.

Six to eighteen months ahead for the best access, and the earlier end for a yacht or a balcony. Monaco's race-week space is fixed: a set number of berths in Port Hercule, a set number of terraces above the track, and a small stock of hotel rooms for the crowd the race draws. Grandstand seats and some hospitality do come back later when earlier bookings fall through, but the best positions rarely do. The 2027 race is on 4–6 June, per the Formula 1 calendar.

The Paddock Club is Formula 1's official hospitality, in suites above the team garages. According to the official Paddock Club page, it includes pit lane walks during the weekend, dining and drinks through the day, and terraces over the pit straight. What it does not include is the rest of the circuit: you see the start, the pit stops and the finish, not the corners. Packages and inclusions differ from race to race, so I check the specific event's terms before recommending one.

A grandstand is closest to the racing, a yacht is the most social, and a balcony gives the most space. Each sees a different short stretch of a 3.337-kilometre lap. A grandstand puts you in the crowd at one corner. A yacht on the trackside quays makes the harbour the venue, with the boat's own hospitality. A balcony looks down on a fixed section with room to move. The choice turns on whether the racing or the setting is the point.

Book at least two days: at Monaco a two- or three-day package takes 10% off each adult ticket. That discount is in the Automobile Club de Monaco's ticket terms, which exclude certain VIP categories and standing areas. In 2027 the case is stronger: Monaco, Monza and Abu Dhabi are all Sprint weekends, so Friday and Saturday both carry sessions that count. Sunday alone suits someone who wants the race and nothing else.

Yes: children aged six to fifteen pay half the adult price for Friday, Saturday or Sunday. That is the rule in the Automobile Club de Monaco's ticket terms, checked October 2026. Children under 15 must be accompanied by an adult and carry ID, and the multi-day package discount does not apply on top of the child price. The reduced rates do not cover every category: the ACM excludes some VIP packages and the standing areas.

Stay in Monaco if walking to your seat matters most; stay along the coast if you want space and a quieter evening. Monaco is small and its roads close around the circuit, so a room in town buys convenience above all. The coast means a daily transfer, and in return a week that is not all track. A common split is race nights in or near Monaco, then a few days further west, which makes it the start of a longer trip through France.

Monza suits a fan who wants to feel the speed; Monaco suits someone for whom the setting is half the point. Monza is built around long straights and outright speed, the tifosi make it the loudest crowd, and the track invasion after the race is something every fan should see once. Monaco is slower and tighter, and unlike any other race for its harbour and terraces. Monza is also the easier trip to extend, with the rest of Italy in its best season immediately afterwards.

Everything runs late: the 2026 race started at 20:00 local time, so the whole trip runs on evening hours. The official 2026 timetable put every session in the afternoon or evening. Travellers from Europe and the Americas can stay on late hours rather than fight the time difference. Heat and humidity stay high after dark, so a hotel close to the circuit park matters, and tickets are sold by zone, which decides how far you can roam.

It depends on the ticket and hospitality terms, which is why I read them before you commit. It does happen: Formula 1 called off the 2023 Emilia Romagna Grand Prix at Imola days before the race because of severe flooding.
