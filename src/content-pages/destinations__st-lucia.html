The Island

The Pitons Are in the South.
So Is the Airport.

St. Lucia's dry season runs December to April, with May as the shoulder; the Atlantic hurricane season runs June 1 to November 30. Long-haul flights land at Hewanorra in the far south, about 90 minutes by road from the north. Choose the base first: Soufrière for the Pitons and small view-led hotels, or the north for long beaches and larger resorts.

The island is 27 miles long and 14 wide (Britannica), but the distances read shorter than they drive. The two Pitons rise side by side from the sea at Soufrière on the southwest coast, inside the Pitons Management Area, which UNESCO inscribed in 2004. That is where the view is, and where most of St. Lucia's small, view-led hotels sit, many of them on steep hillsides with open-walled rooms facing the peaks. The north, around Rodney Bay, Gros Islet and Cap Estate, is flatter and busier, with Reduit Beach, the marinas, restaurants and nightlife that the Saint Lucia Tourism Authority lists for the region, and the larger beachfront resorts, many on all-inclusive plans.

So the first question is not which hotel but which half of the island. A couple who want the Pitons from the bed and a boat to the beach belong in Soufrière. A couple who want to walk into calm water from a sun lounger, with dinner choices on foot, usually belong in the north. A week split between the two is common, and it is the format that makes the most of the 90-minute drive the arrival already demands.

St. Lucia is also the island most often paired with Barbados, whose flight schedule it lacks. That two-island trip has its own page: pairing St. Lucia with Barbados. This page is the single-island week. Seasons, entry rules and transfer times here were checked October 2026; they change, so confirm before booking.

Five Parts of St. Lucia Worth Knowing

Two bases in the southwest, two in the north, and the interior between them. Site facts are from UNESCO and the Saint Lucia Tourism Authority, checked October 2026.

Southwest Coast
Soufrière, the Pitons & Anse Chastanet
Gros Piton (770 m) and Petit Piton (743 m) anchor a 2,909-hectare World Heritage site of land and sea, inscribed in 2004. North of the town, the dark-sand cove at Anse Chastanet is sheltered by cliffs and is one of the island's best snorkeling sites, with the reef starting a few strokes from shore.

North Coast
Rodney Bay & Pigeon Island
The island's busiest resort area: Reduit Beach, Rodney Bay Marina and the Friday-night street party in Gros Islet. At the bay's northern end, Pigeon Island National Landmark keeps the ruins of Fort Rodney and walking trails above the water; it has been joined to the mainland by a causeway since 1972.

Northern Tip
Cap Estate
A former sugar estate north of Pigeon Island, now villas, golf and a handful of resorts, where the Caribbean and the Atlantic meet. The windward side is wild and surf-washed; Cas en Bas on the Atlantic coast is a kite-surfing beach. It suits travelers who want quiet, villa space and a short drive to Rodney Bay's restaurants.

West Coast
Marigot Bay
A deep, green-walled inlet on the west coast between Castries and Soufrière. Its inner harbour, the Hurricane Hole, is protected by steep hills and a narrow entrance, and the marina brings waterfront restaurants. A middle base that shortens the drive to both ends of the island.

The Rainforest Interior
The mountainous centre of the island, with guided trails through the Edmund Forest Reserve to the Enbas Saut waterfalls. Closer to Soufrière are the Diamond Falls gardens and Sulphur Springs, billed as the Caribbean's only drive-in volcano, with steaming vents, bubbling mud pools and warm mineral mud baths.

Three Ways to Shape a St. Lucia Week

One base in the south, one in the north, or both. All three run best in the December–April dry season, with May as the shoulder.

Seven nights in one room with a Pitons view, which is the point of staying here. Arrive at Hewanorra and drive up the southwest coast, or take the helicopter if the flight lands late. Planning Decisions

What Planning Changes on a St. Lucia Trip

Four decisions shape a St. Lucia week, and the first one is made at the airline booking, before any hotel is chosen.

⬡

Which Airport, Which Transfer

Long-haul flights from the US, Canada and Europe use Hewanorra (UVF) at Vieux Fort, on the southern tip. George F. L. Charles (SLU), beside Castries in the north, mostly handles inter-island flights. The Saint Lucia Tourism Authority puts the road between the two at 90 minutes and the helicopter at under 15. For a north-coast hotel, that drive decides whether a late arrival is worth it; Soufrière is closer to Hewanorra.
⬡

View or Beachfront

Around Soufrière the land rises steeply from the sea, so the hotels that face the Pitons are mostly on hillsides, often reached by steep drives and steps, with the beach a shuttle or a boat away. Beachfront rooms with a Pitons view are few. Travellers with mobility limits, or who want to walk straight onto sand, should weigh that before booking the view.
⬡

Boutique or All-Inclusive

The north holds most of the large resorts and most of the all-inclusive rates, including adults-only and family properties. Soufrière's hotels are smaller and mostly sell rooms with breakfast or half board, with meals at the hotel or in town. Neither is better; the choice follows how much the trip should be about the setting and how much about not thinking about the bill. What hotel descriptions actually mean helps when comparing the marketing.
⬡

Booking the Dry Season

December to April is when the Tourism Authority describes the island as driest and busiest, and the holiday weeks from Christmas to New Year fill first. Pitons-view rooms are a small share of the island's inventory, so dates in that window are best fixed early, with the room category chosen before the flights.
When to Go

St. Lucia by Season

Dry Season
December – April
The driest, sunniest and busiest months, per the Saint Lucia Tourism Authority. Trails are firmer for the Gros Piton climb and the sea is usually calm on the west coast. The Christmas and New Year weeks carry the highest rates and the longest minimum stays.
Best Overall Window
Shoulder
May
Still largely dry, quieter and easier to book than the winter months. The Saint Lucia Jazz & Arts Festival ran from April 30 to May 10 in 2026, with open-air stages at Pigeon Island. The argument for these weeks is in the case for shoulder season.
Wet & Hurricane Season
June – November
The National Hurricane Center dates the Atlantic season June 1 to November 30, with the climatological peak around September 10 and most activity from mid-August to mid-October. Showers are heavier in the rainforest than on the coast. Saint Lucia Carnival ran July 1 to 22 in 2026. Flexible bookings and travel insurance matter in these months.

US travelers need a passport but no visa for a tourist stay. Saint Lucia's Department of External Affairs asks visitors for a passport valid for six months and a return ticket. The US State Department's Saint Lucia page also tells travelers to complete an online pre-arrival authorization before flying, and requires a notarized consent letter when a child travels with one parent.

The currency is the Eastern Caribbean dollar, fixed at EC$2.70 to US$1. The Eastern Caribbean Central Bank has held that peg since July 7, 1976. US dollars are widely taken at hotels and in tourist areas, but change usually comes back in EC dollars, so carry some local currency for taxis, markets and the street party.
