Disney and Universal Are Different Trips.
Plan Them That Way.

Walt Disney World has four theme parks and suits children from about age 4; under-3s enter free. Universal Orlando has three theme parks since Epic Universe opened on May 22, 2025, and its headline rides need 40 to 48 inches, so it fits roughly age 8 and up. For both, a sensible starting split is four Disney days, two Universal days and a pool day.

The two resorts share an airport and a climate, and very little else. Walt Disney World spreads four theme parks, two water parks and more than 25 Disney-owned hotels across roughly 40 square miles, linked by its own buses, monorail, boats and the Skyliner gondola. Universal Orlando is compact by comparison: 11 hotels, Universal Studios Florida and Islands of Adventure within walking distance of each other, the Volcano Bay water park beside them, and Epic Universe on its own site a few miles south.

That difference in shape is why the split matters more than the ride list. Disney rewards slow days and an early start at one park; Universal rewards a tight two or three days built around the franchises the family actually cares about. The hotel decides the perks on both sides: Disney's early entry goes to Disney hotel guests, and Universal's Express Unlimited goes only to guests of three specific hotels.

The age of the youngest traveler usually settles the order of operations, which is the same logic behind how we plan family trips by age. Orlando is also one of the few places where multigenerational trips work without compromise, provided the grandparents' days are planned as carefully as the children's. Park rules, heights and hotel benefits on this page were checked October 2026; they change, so confirm before booking.

The Decision

Disney or Universal? How to Split the Week

Six questions settle it, in roughly this order. Heights come from Universal's ride height requirements; line-skipping and hotel rules from Disney's Lightning Lane page and Universal's Early Park Admission page. Checked October 2026.

01
Ages & Heights
Disney works from about age 4, and children under 3 enter free. Universal's big rides are height-gated: Mario Kart: Bowser's Challenge from 40 inches, Hagrid's and Stardust Racers from 48. Measure the children before choosing the split, not at the gate.
02
Line-Skipping
At Disney, Lightning Lane Multi Pass and Single Pass are bought per day, at prices that vary by date. At Universal, Express is bought per day too, unless you stay at one of the three hotels that include Express Unlimited. Epic Universe sells its own Express separately.
03
The Hotel Perk
Every Disney hotel guest gets in 30 minutes early at every theme park, every day. Universal's on-site guests get Early Park Admission up to an hour before opening, at the parks and rides Universal names for that period. The longer head start is Universal's.
04
How Many Park Days
Four Disney parks in four days is a sprint, so a pool day goes in the middle. Universal's three parks need two days with Express Unlimited and three without it. Epic Universe always takes its own day.
05
Doing Both
Put Universal first or last, never in the middle, and change hotels once. Two hotel stays mean each resort's perks apply on the days spent there, which is the only way to get both early entries in one week.
06
Teens vs Young Kids
Young children pull toward Disney's characters and dark rides; teenagers pull toward Universal's coasters and franchises. A mixed-age family usually means both, with the Universal days built around the oldest child.

What We Add to Orlando Planning

An Orlando week is decided by a handful of dates and rules set weeks before arrival. Planning is mostly getting those in the right order.

⬡
Booking Windows
Disney hotel guests can book dining 60 days before arrival for the whole stay, per Disney's dining FAQ, and can buy Lightning Lane 7 days before arrival instead of the 3 days everyone else gets. Those dates go in the plan the day the hotel is booked.
⬡
Tickets Matched to the Plan
A one-park-per-day ticket, a Park Hopper and a Universal park-to-park ticket each suit a different itinerary. Buying the ticket after the day-by-day plan exists, rather than before, avoids paying for flexibility the week will never use.
⬡
Hotel Chosen by Perk
On both sides the hotel decides more than the room. A Disney deluxe adds Extended Evening Hours; three Universal hotels add Express Unlimited. Whether those are worth the rate depends on the park days, the ages and the dates, so the comparison is done per trip.
⬡
Pacing for Heat and Rain
Central Florida summers bring heat and frequent afternoon storms. Days built around early starts, a midday break at the hotel and a return in the evening hold up better than a rope-drop-to-fireworks plan, especially with young children or grandparents.
When to Go

Orlando by Season

Mid-Winter
January – February
Outside the New Year and Presidents' Day weeks, the most comfortable stretch for walking parks all day: mild, drier and before spring break. Some attractions close for refurbishment in these months, so check the schedule before fixing dates.
Best Overall Window
Spring
March – May
March and Easter fill with school spring breaks. Late April and May, once those end and before summer vacation starts, are a strong second choice: warm, still mostly dry, and quieter than the weeks either side.
Summer
June – August
School holidays, heat and near-daily afternoon thunderstorms. Workable with early starts and a midday break. The National Hurricane Center dates the Atlantic hurricane season from June 1 to November 30.
Autumn & Holidays
September – December
September through mid-November is the other quiet window once schools are back, at the cost of peak hurricane months; flexible bookings and travel insurance matter here. Thanksgiving and Christmas to New Year are among the busiest weeks of the year.
