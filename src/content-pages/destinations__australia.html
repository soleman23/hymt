The Continent

Australia Is Four Trips
People Book as One.

Australia covers 7.7 million square kilometres, and Sydney, Uluru, the Great Barrier Reef and Tasmania are four separate trips joined by domestic flights. Two regions need 11–13 days; three need 16–18. May through September suits the Red Centre and the tropical north; December through February suits Sydney and Tasmania. Almost nothing suits both.

The number worth holding on to is the one Geoscience Australia publishes: 7,688,287 square kilometres of land. Held against a fourteen-day holiday, that figure is the whole problem. Sydney, Cairns, Uluru and Hobart are not stops on a circuit, and each move between them is a domestic sector plus the half-day at either end that nobody writes into the plan. Four of those in a fortnight produces a trip spent in terminals.

The second thing that goes wrong is the calendar, because Australia has several. Sydney, Melbourne and Tasmania are at their best from December through February. The Red Centre and tropical Queensland are at their worst in precisely those months: Parks Australia closes the Valley of the Winds walk at Kata Tjuta beyond the first lookout when the forecast reaches 36°C, and the tropical north spends those weeks in the monsoon with marine stingers inshore. Flip to May through September and the position reverses. No month serves the whole country. The first question is not when to go; it is which half of the country the trip is about.

The reef question has a real answer too, and it is not Cairns by default. Cairns is the working port: the liveaboards that run the northern Ribbon Reefs and the Coral Sea sail from there, and if you dive, that is where you belong. Port Douglas is the better base for everyone else. And this is not the trip for someone with ten days who wants to see Australia. With ten days you see one part of it properly. The version reaching for three comes home with photographs of four airports.

The Kimberley · Australia
April – September · 12–14 Days
The Kimberley by Expedition Ship
The one region of Australia where the vessel is the itinerary. Expedition ships work the coast between Broome and Darwin through the dry months, and the small-ship end of that fleet — thirty to a hundred guests, tenders and helicopters aboard — reaches places no land trip does: Horizontal Falls in Talbot Bay on the right tide, Montgomery Reef emerging as the water drains off it, and rock art reached on foot from a beach landing. Two nights in Broome at either end for Cable Beach and the pearling history. This is the trip for people who have already done the cities, and the one with the narrowest season and the fewest berths.

What We Add to Australia Planning

Australia is the destination where the shape of the itinerary matters more than any single booking inside it, and where that shape is decided by the domestic air schedule and the calendar.

⬡
Four Trips, Not One Loop
The failure mode is always the same: one region too many. Every extra region is a domestic sector, an airport morning, a check-in at the far end and a day that reads as travel rather than travelling. We will argue you down to two regions on a twelve-day trip every time, and we would rather cut Uluru than cut the spare day on the reef. Sequence matters as much as the list — which region opens the trip and takes the jet lag, and which sector runs thin enough that a delay eats a night. We build the ground arrangements around the air schedule.
⬡
Which Reef Gateway, and Which Boat
"The Great Barrier Reef" is not one place with one condition. The Great Barrier Reef Marine Park Authority manages roughly 344,400 square kilometres of it, and the difference between an inshore fringing reef and the outer Ribbon Reefs is the difference between a disappointing day and the one you flew for. That makes the operator the real decision: where the boat goes, how many people it carries, whether it moves site when conditions turn, and whether there is a marine biologist aboard or a public address system.
⬡
Uluru With Anangu Guides
Uluru-Kata Tjuta National Park is jointly managed by its Anangu traditional owners and Parks Australia, and the climb closed permanently in October 2019 at the traditional owners' request. What visitors do instead is the part worth planning. A coach commentary at a sunset viewing area and a dawn walk on the base circuit with an Anangu guide are not two versions of one morning — the first is a photograph of a rock, the second is the reason the park is listed for cultural as well as natural criteria. Guiding at that level is limited and committed early, particularly through the winter months.
⬡
The Bookings That Close First
Australia has a short list of things that sell out long before the hotels do, and each one is trip-defining. Walker bookings on Tasmania's Overland Track are capped and administered by Tasmania Parks and Wildlife Service through the summer season. Kimberley expedition ships run one season a year across a small fleet, and the good cabins go a year out. Coral Sea liveaboard cabins and Anangu-guided walk slots behave the same way. These are the bookings we open a file with; hotels follow them.
When to Go

Australia by Season

Dry Season, North and Centre
May – September
The window that makes a multi-region trip possible at all. Tropical Queensland is dry and outside the inshore stinger season, the Red Centre is walkable at midday, and the Kimberley ships are running. Two caveats: nights at Uluru fall below freezing in June and July, and the south-east trades blow hardest through the same months, which is what puts outer-reef boats on weather watch. Within the window, May and September give you least of both problems. Dwarf minke whales pass the northern Ribbon Reefs in June and July, the one good reason to accept the wind.
Best Overall Window
Southern Summer
December – February
The right months for the southern half and the wrong ones for everything north of Brisbane. Sydney, Melbourne, the Great Ocean Road and Tasmania are at their peak — long days, swimmable water, the Overland Track in its walking season. Meanwhile the Red Centre passes 40°C often enough that walks close on the forecast, and tropical Queensland is in the monsoon with stingers inshore and cyclone risk on the coast. For southern cities and Tasmania, this is the window and it is not close.
The Shoulder Months
March – April & October – November
The compromise window, and the honest answer for anyone determined to cross the country in one trip. March and April catch the end of the wet in the north; October and November get ahead of the stinger season while Sydney warms. Rates sit below the July peak in the north and the Christmas peak in the south. The trade is variability — an April reef day can be flat calm or cancelled, and this is the window where the spare day earns its place.

Seasonal notes and park rules checked August 2026 against the Bureau of Meteorology, Parks Australia and Tasmania Parks and Wildlife Service. Walk closures, booking seasons and stinger advice change; confirm before booking.

What Australia Actually Costs

Priced per region, plus the flights between them

What actually sets the number
  • How many regions — the Uluru-Kata Tjuta pass is A$38 for three days, and each extra region adds a sector

Common Questions

Australia — FAQ

The questions that come up most when planning a first Australia trip — how many days it really needs, which reef gateway, what happened to the Uluru climb, and whether Tasmania belongs in the same itinerary.

May through September, if the Red Centre or tropical Queensland is on the itinerary. Those are the dry months in the north, the walkable ones in the desert, and they fall outside the inshore stinger season. The cost is Sydney, Melbourne and Tasmania in their winter at the same time. If the trip is southern cities and Tasmania only, invert it and travel December through February. If it has to be both halves, September and April or May serve them least badly; put the reef days at the front of the stay. Season detail by region comes from the Bureau of Meteorology.

Eleven to thirteen days for two regions, and sixteen to eighteen for three. Below eleven days, pick one region and do it properly. Sydney plus the Great Barrier Reef is the two-region trip most people mean: four nights Sydney, five in the tropical north. Adding the Red Centre pushes the minimum to sixteen, because Uluru deserves three nights and the sector in and out of Ayers Rock costs most of a day at each end. Tasmania is a fourth trip rather than a fourth stop. Twelve days carrying three regions and four domestic sectors is the version that comes apart, usually around day six.

Port Douglas if you are snorkelling, Cairns if you are diving from a liveaboard. Port Douglas sits closest to the Agincourt ribbon reefs on the outer edge of the continental shelf, which the day boats reach in a morning, and it is the better town for five nights. Cairns is the working port: the liveaboards that run the northern Ribbon Reefs and the Coral Sea sail from there. The Whitsundays are a sailing destination with reef access rather than a reef destination. Whichever you pick, the operator matters more than the town — the Great Barrier Reef Marine Park Authority manages roughly 344,400 square kilometres, and no single day out represents it.

No longer — the climb closed permanently on 26 October 2019 at the request of the Anangu traditional owners. They jointly manage Uluru-Kata Tjuta National Park with Parks Australia, and the date fell on the anniversary of the 1985 handback. The base walk of roughly ten kilometres passes waterholes, rock art and sheltered faces the climbing route ignored, and it is the walk to do at first light with an Anangu guide. Uluru rises about 348 metres above the plain and reads very differently from underneath. Walk closures, hours and heat rules come from Parks Australia; confirm before booking, because walks close on the forecast rather than on the day.

Yes, on the outer reef with an operator that moves sites, and not on an inshore pontoon. The reef has been through repeated mass bleaching events, and the coverage has flattened a system the size of a small country into one verdict. Condition varies across it: the northern Ribbon Reefs and the outer Agincourt reefs are not the inshore fringing reefs that photograph worst. It is surveyed and published sector by sector by the Great Barrier Reef Marine Park Authority and the Australian Institute of Marine Science rather than as one national figure. Ask where the boat is going and how many people it takes.

Yes — almost every visitor needs an ETA or an eVisitor authorisation, applied for online before departure. Which one depends on the passport: United States and Canadian passport holders generally use the Electronic Travel Authority, while most United Kingdom and European Union passports use eVisitor. Neither is issued on arrival. Categories, conditions and processing times change, so check the requirement against the current rules rather than against what a previous traveller did. The authoritative source is the Australian Department of Home Affairs. Apply before flights are ticketed: a delayed authorisation against a fixed departure is the one problem an advisor cannot solve for you.

Not as an add-on. Tasmania is its own trip. Bolting it onto a Sydney-and-reef trip buys a domestic sector each way for two or three nights, which is not enough time to leave Hobart. On its own, eleven to thirteen days covers Hobart and the Tasman Peninsula, Freycinet, the Bay of Fires and Cradle Mountain. The Tasmanian Wilderness World Heritage Area runs to about 1.58 million hectares, and the Overland Track covers 65 kilometres from Cradle Mountain to Lake St Clair over about six days, with walker bookings capped through the summer season by Tasmania Parks and Wildlife Service. Travel December through March.

Both, in different places. Australia is one of the easiest countries in the world to drive: sealed roads, signage in English, long distances that are well served. Tasmania and the Great Ocean Road are better self-driven, because the unplanned stops are the point and a schedule spoils them. Uluru is the opposite case: the Anangu-guided base walk is the reason to be there, and no self-drive version reaches it.
