The Country

Colombia Is South America's
Shortest Way In.

Colombia's driest months are December through March, with a second dry window in July and August. A first trip runs 9–12 days: three nights in the coffee region, three in Medellín or Bogotá, and two or three in Cartagena. Bogotá sits at 2,640 metres and Cartagena at sea level, so the country has no single climate.

Colombia is the top corner of the continent. Punta Gallinas in La Guajira is the northernmost point of the South American mainland, and Colombia is the only country there with coastlines on both the Caribbean and the Pacific. For anyone starting from the eastern side of North America, that geography is the whole argument: the continent without the transatlantic-length haul, and the rare South American trip that still works on nine days rather than sixteen.

Cartagena is the reason most people book Colombia, and a week in Cartagena is the least interesting week the country offers. The walled city is genuinely first rank — the port, fortresses and monuments were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1984, and Castillo San Felipe de Barajas is the largest Spanish fortification in the Americas. It is also about a square kilometre. Two or three nights reads it properly. Seven turns into beach days on water the Magdalena keeps cloudy, a trip most people could have had closer to home.

The trip is in the Andes. Medellín sits near 1,500 metres, the coffee belt runs roughly 1,200 to 2,000, and Bogotá is at 2,640 — more than a kilometre above Denver. That vertical spread is the product. A shirt on a Cartagena rooftop, a fleece on a Bogotá street and a hammock on a working coffee farm, inside one week. Plan Colombia by altitude rather than by map distance and the itinerary writes itself.

Chocó & the Pacific · Colombia
July – October · 9–11 Days
Whales on the Chocó Coast
A second Colombia trip rather than a first, and the one I would argue hardest for. Humpbacks calve along the Pacific coast roughly July through October, and the lodges around Nuquí and Bahía Solano put you on a boat among them with no other vessel in sight. Getting there means a small aircraft and no road, no air conditioning, generator power, and rain that arrives sideways. Four or five nights is right. Pair it with three in Medellín on the way through and three in the coffee region afterwards, which puts the hot shower at the end rather than the start. Not for anyone who needs a lobby, and the trip clients still talk about years later.

What We Add to Colombia Planning

The useful information about Colombia is granular — a neighbourhood, a road, a month, a family — and almost none of it is on a booking site.

⬡
Where a Guide Is Not Optional
Most of what a visitor does in Colombia now needs no more caution than any large city anywhere. Three things still want a local fixer. Comuna 13 is worth a morning, with a guide who grew up in it. The Ciudad Perdida trek is only legal with an authorised operator. And ground movement outside the main corridors after dark is a question to ask before you book. We do not send anyone where our partners will not go, and we say so.
⬡
One Country, Four Weather Systems
Colombia straddles the equator, so its wet and drier periods do not line up between regions. The Andean interior's dry windows are December to March and July to August; the Caribbean coast's is December to April; the Pacific is wet year-round. That is why "which Colombia am I going to" is the better question, and getting it backwards is how people end up walking Cocora in cloud.
⬡
Fincas That Are Still Farms
"Coffee farm stay" in Colombia covers a family finca still harvesting its own lot, a former farmhouse run as a small hotel with a demonstration mill, and a new build with a coffee bag in the lobby. The first is one of the best stays in South America; the third is a hotel with a theme. What separates them is whether the person walking you through the mill sells that coffee. Colombia grows only Arabica, and the Federación Nacional de Cafeteros publishes harvest timing by region. Knowing which fortnight you book into separates a working farm from a set.
⬡
The Drives That Should Be Flights
Colombia is mountainous in a way distance maps do not show. The commonest self-planned error is a road day that should have been a flight. Bogotá to the coffee region, Medellín to the coast, anything to Cartagena — those are flights. What works on the ground is short and local: Salento to the Cocora valley is about 11 kilometres and half an hour in a Willys jeep, Santa Marta to the Tayrona entrance around an hour, Medellín to Guatapé roughly two. Medellín's airport sits above the city at around 2,100 metres in Rionegro, and the drive down into the valley takes about 45 minutes. Build the ground schedule around the flight schedule, never the reverse.

When to Go

Colombia by Season

The Long Dry Window
December – March
The one window where the Andes and the Caribbean coast are both at their best, which is why a Bogotá–coffee–Medellín–Cartagena trip is easiest to build here. The trade is that this is also Colombia's own holiday season: the last two weeks of December and the first of January are the busiest and most expensive of the year on the coast, and the good fincas in Quindío are committed months ahead. February and March are the quieter half of the same window and, on balance, the better booking.
Best Overall Window
The Second Dry Spell
July – August
The Andean interior gets a second drier run through July and August, and it brings two things December does not. Humpback whales are calving on the Pacific coast from roughly July into October. And Medellín's Feria de las Flores fills the city in early August, either the best week to be there or the worst, depending on whether you want a parade of flower-carrying silleteros outside your hotel. The coast is wetter and more humid than in February.
The Wet Months
April – May & October – November
The two rainy spells, and not the write-off they sound like. Rain in the Colombian Andes usually means a heavy afternoon rather than a lost day, and rates across the interior drop from the December and July peaks. October and November are the worst months on the Caribbean coast and the wrong time for a Cartagena-and-Tayrona trip. April and May are a reasonable gamble for an Andes-only itinerary with slack in the schedule. Barranquilla's Carnival falls in the four days before Ash Wednesday and moves with the church calendar.

Two or three, not a week. The walled city is about a square kilometre and genuinely first rank — UNESCO inscribed the port, fortresses and monuments in 1984, and Castillo San Felipe de Barajas is the largest Spanish fortress in the Americas. In that climate the useful hours are before nine in the morning and after seven at night, and the water immediately offshore carries river sediment. Three nights lets you walk the walls at dawn twice, eat properly in Getsemaní, and take one boat day if you want one. The days you free up belong in the coffee region.

Three nights, and yes, if you walk it rather than photograph it. Base near Salento or Filandia, each about an hour by road from Armenia or Pereira. UNESCO inscribed the Coffee Cultural Landscape in 2011 for the settlement and farming pattern itself. The Cocora valley is roughly 11 kilometres and half an hour from Salento by jeep, and its wax palms — Ceroxylon quindiuense, Colombia's national tree and the tallest palm in the world at up to 60 metres — stand in open pasture rather than forest. Go at opening. By late morning the cloud is up the valley and the car park is full; people who call Cocora overrated are describing eleven o'clock.

For some people yes: Bogotá sits at about 2,640 metres, high enough to notice on the first day. That is more than a kilometre above Denver. Expect a shorter breath on stairs, a poor first night's sleep and a low tolerance for alcohol. Two easy days at the start handle it. Monserrate, at 3,152 metres directly above the old town, is worth taking the funicular up rather than walking on day one. Medellín is different: near 1,500 metres, it holds days in the high twenties Celsius and nights in the high teens all year, which is the eternal-spring line.
