The Region

The Most Culturally Rich
Region in the World

Asia is not one season. Japan works late March to May and October to November, Southeast Asia's dry season runs November to April, and India and South Asia run October to March. A first trip needs 10–14 days across one or two countries rather than four. Japan and Thailand are the two most common first choices, for opposite reasons.

Japan alone is enough to justify the flight. The shun concept — eating in strict accordance with what the season makes available — runs through every level of Japanese food culture from a ramen shop in Fukuoka to an omakase counter in Tokyo where the chef has bought from the same fish supplier for eighteen years. A private tea ceremony with a practitioner who trained for fifteen years is accessible to a traveler who knows to ask. Fushimi Inari before dawn, when the thousand torii gates are yours alone. These are not secrets — they are simply available only to travelers who planned that way.

The rest of the continent is equally layered. Bali's spiritual geography — the orientation of every temple, every compound, every offering — is comprehensible to a traveler who has a guide who understands Tri Hita Karana. Thailand's hill tribe villages and the Chiang Mai cooking culture that predates the tourist food schools by four hundred years. Vietnam's Hoi An tailors and the Central Highlands coffee country. India's architecture, its textile traditions, its Ayurvedic pharmacology — a continent in itself.

We plan Asia around depth rather than distance covered. Fewer places, longer stays, specialists instead of generalists, and itineraries built around the specific experiences that produce the kind of travel that stays with a person.

When to Go

Asia by Season

Japan Window
March – May & October – November
The two best Japan windows — cherry blossom season from late March through early April (the single most in-demand travel window in Asia, requiring advance planning) and autumn foliage from late October through November. Both windows produce extraordinary color, ideal temperatures, and a Japan that actively rewards being there. Hotels and ryokans in Kyoto during sakura season sell out four to six months in advance — plan accordingly.
Best Japan Window
Southeast Asia Dry Season
November – April
The primary window for Thailand, Vietnam, Cambodia, Bali, and Sri Lanka — dry, warm, and consistently excellent across the entire region. December through February is peak season with the highest rates. October and November (shoulder into dry season) offer similar conditions with lower occupancy. The wet season from May through October brings rain but also dramatically lower rates and genuinely beautiful landscapes — manageable for experienced travelers with flexibility.
India & South Asia
October – March
The Indian subcontinent in summer is hot and during monsoon season (June through September) genuinely uncomfortable for most travel. October through March is the primary window — cool in the north, manageable in Rajasthan, and still warm in Kerala and the south. The Diwali festival window in October or November is extraordinary for Rajasthan, with fort illuminations and a cultural energy that no other season provides. Avoid April through June in northern India entirely.

Asia — FAQ

Questions about planning Asia travel — from first-time destination selection to timing, itinerary depth, and what makes each country worth the long-haul flight.

Japan and Thailand are the two most common answers for different reasons. Japan delivers a consistent, high-quality, safe, and logistically manageable experience with extraordinarily high reward — food, culture, service, and aesthetics all at exceptional levels. Thailand combines beach, temples, food culture, and accessibility at a lower price point. Both are excellent starting points.

Japan as a standalone works in 10–14 days. Southeast Asia combinations — Thailand plus Bali, or Vietnam plus Cambodia — work well in 14–18 days. India deserves at least two weeks for a single-region trip; trying to cover too much produces a rushed experience. The more meaningful question is depth versus breadth — I almost always recommend going deeper into fewer places.

India is best October–February. Bali's shoulder months — May–June and September–October — are often ideal. I plan timing specifically for each destination to avoid monsoon conditions without paying peak prices.
