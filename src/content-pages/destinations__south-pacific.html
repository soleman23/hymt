The South Pacific rewards travelers who commit to it. French Polynesia's overwater bungalows above a lagoon in Bora Bora are genuinely one of the finest resort experiences available anywhere — but the category spans from extraordinary to merely expensive, and the property selection matters as much here as anywhere. Fiji's private island resorts are a category of their own: a handful of properties on uninhabited islands where the staff-to-guest ratio is so favorable that the experience feels like a private charter. New Zealand's South Island is one of the great adventure and landscape destinations in the world — the Milford Track, the Otago Rail Trail, the Southern Lakes.

Australia brings its own dimension — the Great Barrier Reef by liveaboard rather than day trip, the Kimberley coast by expedition vessel, the Daintree Rainforest and Cape Tribulation, Sydney for a city that has somehow kept a genuine harbor at its center. The Cook Islands for the most intimate overwater bungalow experience in the Pacific, on a lagoon that has fewer flights in per week than Bora Bora has in a day.

We plan South Pacific trips around the commitment they require — which means getting every element right, from the overwater villa category to the inter-island logistics to the marine activity scheduling that makes a reef trip genuinely exceptional rather than just wet.

Overwater Villa Selection
Not all overwater bungalows are equal — and in French Polynesia especially, villa placement over the lagoon versus the outer reef, the quality of the lagoon floor below the deck, and the privacy from neighboring villas are highly variable across properties at similar price points. Island Combination Logic
French Polynesia has 118 islands. The Air Tahiti network connects about a dozen of them on a schedule that doesn't always accommodate the itinerary travelers plan. Knowing which islands to combine, which connections are reliable, and which sequences work against the prevailing wind patterns is the planning work that determines whether a multi-island itinerary flows or fractures. We've built enough of these itineraries to know exactly how to sequence them.

Marine Activity Guidance
The South Pacific is a diving and snorkeling destination above all else — and the quality of those experiences varies enormously by reef health, guide expertise, and timing. When to Go

South Pacific by Season

Dry Season
May – October
The primary South Pacific window — dry, calm, and consistently excellent for French Polynesia, Fiji, and Vanuatu. Trade winds keep temperatures comfortable. Underwater visibility peaks in the dry season across most reefs. This is also the Southern Hemisphere winter, which makes New Zealand's South Island ski season and Australia's outback travel optimal. The peak travel window for French Polynesia and Fiji — book overwater villas and private island properties at least four to six months in advance for June through August.
Best Overall Window
Wet Season
November – April
The Southern Hemisphere summer — hot and humid in Fiji and French Polynesia, with a cyclone risk from December through March that is real but manageable with proper travel insurance and flexible dates. Rates drop significantly. New Zealand and Australia are at their best in these months — warm temperatures, long days, and the full range of outdoor activities. Australia's Great Barrier Reef diving is excellent year-round; November through January brings warm surface temperatures and calm Coral Sea conditions.
Shoulder Months
April – May & October – November
The two shoulder windows between wet and dry seasons offer some of the best value in the South Pacific — rates 20–30% below peak, weather that is generally excellent, and occupancy levels that make private island resorts feel genuinely private. April and May are particularly strong for Fiji (entering dry season, minimal crowds) and for Australia's northern Queensland (end of wet season, before the winter rush). October and November work well for New Zealand, with spring wildflowers and lower rates than December through February.

The right choice depends on whether you want a resort experience or a travel experience.
