The Region

Three Nights Is the Trip.
Two Weeks Is a Stretch.

The Gulf works from November to March, when Dubai and Abu Dhabi sit in the mid-twenties and everything outdoors is possible. From May to September daily highs pass 40°C and the whole day moves inside. Three or four days covers Dubai properly; a two-week Gulf-only holiday runs out of material. Abu Dhabi to Dubai is about 140 km.

The highest-value version of this region is three nights on the way to somewhere else. Dubai and Doha were built as connecting hubs and they are extremely good at it — a long-haul journey that would otherwise be twenty hours of airports becomes two nights of hotel, a morning of architecture and a dinner, and you arrive in Asia or Africa having slept. That is not a consolation prize. It is the single best use of the Gulf, and the reason I book more UAE nights than almost any other destination while recommending very few two-week Gulf holidays.

The three cities are not interchangeable. Dubai does scale and appetite — the Council on Tall Buildings and Urban Habitat lists the Burj Khalifa at 828 metres — plus the best restaurant depth in the region and a creek where trading dhows still load by hand. Abu Dhabi does culture and calm: the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque and Louvre Abu Dhabi on Saadiyat, and beaches that are not a construction site. Doha does museums, and it does them at a level Dubai has not matched.

Who it is wrong for: anyone who wants old towns, mountain villages and archaeology within a short drive. That trip exists on the same peninsula and it is Oman — wadis, forts, a fjord coastline and a road network built for it. Anyone travelling between May and September should also reconsider. The heat is not an inconvenience to be worked around with early starts; it is a wall, and the honest planning answer is a different month or a different country.

The Gulf and the Empty Quarter
The week above with the desert taken seriously instead of sampled. Three nights Dubai, then Abu Dhabi for three, then roughly 240 km southwest to the Liwa for two nights on the edge of the Rub' al Khali — dunes in the hundreds of metres, a night sky the coast cannot offer, and mornings cool enough to walk a ridge. Add a night in Ras Al Khaimah on the way back for the Jebel Jais road and the Hajar escarpment. November to February only: this itinerary depends on being outdoors at midday, and from March onward it stops working. Two desert nights, never one, or the drive eats the experience it was meant to deliver.

Gulf & Oman · Arabia
October – March · 11–13 Days
The Gulf Into Oman
For travellers who wanted the Gulf to have landscape and history, this is the fix, and it is a border crossing rather than a compromise. Three nights Dubai and two Abu Dhabi for the architecture and the museums, then cross into Oman for six or seven: Muscat's souq and the Grand Mosque, the mountain road to Nizwa and Jabreen for the forts, a wadi day, and two nights in the Wahiba Sands. The contrast is the point — the Emirates show you what was built in fifty years, Oman shows you what was there before. Book the crossing and the vehicle properly; the paperwork is routine but it is not something to improvise at the border.

What We Add to Gulf Planning

The Gulf is the destination where the brochure and the experience diverge fastest, and where the four decisions below account for almost all of the difference.

⬡
Which Address You Sleep At
Dubai is a forty-kilometre strip, not a city centre, and the address decides the trip. Downtown puts you at the tower and the restaurants and nowhere near a swimmable beach. The Marina and Jumeirah beach strip put you in the water and forty minutes from the old town in traffic. Al Fahidi and the creek side is where the history is and where the hotel stock is thinnest. Pick wrong and the week is spent in a car. Most of what a booking site describes as central is not central to anything you came for.
⬡
The Stopover, Engineered
A stopover is a scheduling problem before it is a hotel problem. It turns on which carrier's routing gives you a civilised arrival hour rather than a 3am landing, whether a day room or a late checkout is the cheaper fix for a night departure, and how far the hotel sits from the terminal you actually leave from — a detail that changes with the airline, not the city. The second night is what converts the exercise, because one night is a shower and a nap. Get those decisions right and a stopover reads as a short holiday. Get them wrong and you have paid for a hotel to sleep through jet lag in.
⬡
Desert Camps, Sorted Honestly
There are three products sold under one word. The coach-park version is an afternoon of dune bashing, a buffet under floodlights and a bus home, and it is what most people book. The second is a permanent camp inside the Dubai Desert Conservation Reserve, 225 km² of protected dune within an hour of the city, where the vehicle numbers are capped. The third is two nights out in the Liwa on the Empty Quarter's edge, which is the real thing and costs a full day of driving. We say which is which before you book, not after.
⬡
The Calendar Nobody Checks
Four things move Gulf dates and none of them appear on a booking engine. Ramadan shifts about eleven days earlier each Gregorian year and changes daytime hours everywhere; the UAE Government portal publishes current guidance. Eid fills domestic hotels. The Abu Dhabi Grand Prix at Yas Marina takes late-season rates vertical across both emirates. And the trade-fair calendar can double a Dubai room rate on a Tuesday in February for reasons no leisure traveller ever sees coming. We check all four before proposing dates.
When to Go

The Gulf by Season

Cool Season
November – March
The only window in which the outdoor half of the region exists. Daytime sits in the mid-twenties, evenings on a terrace are pleasant rather than survivable, and the desert nights turn genuinely cold — a jacket in the Liwa in January is not optional. Everything else follows from that: the mosque at golden hour, the corniche walk, the Jebel Jais road, two nights on a dune. It is also peak season, so the good desert camps and the beach properties in Abu Dhabi commit months ahead, and Christmas and New Year sit at the top of the rate card.
Best Overall Window
Shoulder
April & October
Two useful months either side, and they are not equivalent. April is the safer bet: the heat builds through the month, and the first half still supports a morning outdoors before it becomes an indoor day. October is the harder call, because the sea has absorbed a summer and the humidity lingers well after the air temperature drops. Take either for a city-and-hotel trip, where a pool and a museum carry the day. Do not take either for a desert itinerary — that is a November-to-February trip and pushing it costs you the thing you came for.
Summer
May – September
Genuinely unusable outdoors, and I would rather say so than sell it. Dubai and Doha run daily highs above 40°C for months at a stretch, with Gulf humidity on top; climate normals are published by the WMO World Weather Information Service. The city functions entirely indoors — malls, museums, hotel interiors — and the rates fall accordingly, which is the only argument for going. If a summer trip is fixed by school dates, it is a hotel holiday with air conditioning between destinations, and it should be planned as one rather than dressed up as sightseeing.

Cover shoulders and knees in public buildings, dress as you like inside hotels and resorts, and follow the posted rules at mosques. Malls and souks expect modest dress from everyone; beaches and hotel pools do not. Women visiting the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque need a headscarf and full-length sleeves, and abayas are lent at the entrance.
