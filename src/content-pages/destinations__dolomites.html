The Range

One Range, Two High Routes,
and a Short Summer

The Dolomites are best walked hut to hut on the Alta Via 1, a 125 km, 12-stage high route from Lago di Braies south to Belluno, or on the longer, more exposed Alta Via 2. The huts open from June to September, and the end of June to the end of September is the recommended window. Most walkers arrive through Innsbruck or Venice and leave through Venice.

The Dolomites are a UNESCO World Heritage Site, inscribed in 2009 as nine separate mountain systems covering 141,903 hectares, with 18 peaks above 3,000 metres (UNESCO World Heritage Centre, listing 1237). Ten signposted high routes, the Alte Vie, cross them. Two matter for a first trek. The Alta Via 1 is the classic: the Cortina tourism board measures it at about 125 km and 7,300 m of climbing in 12 stages, walked in 10 to 15 days end to end. South Tyrol's tourism board, which counts the variants, puts it at 150 km in 13 days and calls it the less strenuous of the two, with only short via ferrata sections. The Alta Via 2, from Bressanone to Feltre, is 185 km in 15 days by the same board's count, with grade III scrambling and a glacier stage on the Marmolada.

A night in a rifugio, a staffed mountain hut, is the reason the format works. Dinner, bed and breakfast are sold together as half board, so a walker carries clothing and water rather than a stove and food. The Club Alpino Italiano's rules for its own huts make a personal sheet sleeping bag compulsory (CAI hut regulations), and its 2025 tariff circular gives members of CAI and reciprocal alpine clubs a guaranteed minimum 20% discount on half board (CAI tariff circular 2025). Many huts on the routes are privately run and set their own terms.

The season is short because the huts are high. The Alta Via 1's opening stage climbs 900 m from Lago di Braies (1,494 m) to Rifugio Biella at 2,327 m (stage 1, Cortina tourism board), and South Tyrol describes the main line at an average height of 2,000 m. Infodolomiti, the Belluno Dolomites tourism portal, gives the hut opening period as June to September and recommends the end of June to the end of September. Checked October 2026.

Trip Formats

Three Ways to Plan a Dolomites Trip

The choice is between moving every night and staying put. All three formats run inside the same hut season, from late June to September.

Late June – September · 6–7 Nights
Hut to Hut: The Northern Alta Via 1
The section most trekkers choose, walked north to south as the route is signed. The Cortina tourism board's day plan runs Braies to Rifugio Biella, then Rifugio Fanes, Rifugio Lagazuoi, Rifugio Nuvolau and Rifugio Croda da Lago, before the line continues toward the Civetta. A typical stage is 3.5 to 6 hours of walking. Arrive through Innsbruck or Venice and the Puster Valley; leave from Cortina, a two-hour direct bus from Venice airport, or carry on south toward Belluno.
Rifugi Nightly Half Board North to South
Late June – September · 7 Nights
Base-Camp Week: Val Gardena & Alta Badia
One or two valley hotels and a different day hike each morning, using lifts to start high: the Seceda ridge, the Puez plateau, the Sella and a lunch at a hut instead of a night in one. A day on a via ferrata with a certified mountain guide fits in midweek. The format suits mixed-fitness groups and anyone who wants the scenery without changing beds nightly.
One Base Lift-Assisted Guided Ferrata
Late June – September · 9–10 Nights
Dolomites, Then Venice
A shorter hut-to-hut section or a Cortina base, then down to the lagoon for the last nights, since Venice is the usual departure airport anyway. For the city half, see the Italy guide to Venice, Tuscany and Rome; for why fewer stops make the better trip, read the European grand tour mistake.

Planning Decisions

What Planning Changes on a Dolomites Trek

A hut-to-hut trek is a chain of bookings that each depend on the one before. Four decisions shape it.

⬡

Booking the Hut Chain

Every night is a separate booking with a separate hut, and one full hut breaks the sequence. CAI huts take reservations through the club's hut booking portal; privately run huts take them directly. July and August, the heart of the season, are when the popular huts fill first, so the order of work is dates, then the hardest hut to get, then the rest of the chain around it.
⬡

Pack or Transfer

Most of the high route is reached only on foot, so the standard plan is a light pack carried the whole way: clothing, a sheet sleeping bag, rain shell and water. Where a hut sits near a road or lift, a bag can sometimes be moved by vehicle, but that is arranged hut by hut and is not something to assume. The base-camp week removes the question entirely.
⬡

Via Ferrata, Properly Equipped

A via ferrata is a cabled climbing route. Cortina's tourism board lists the kit as helmet, harness, gloves and a via ferrata set, asks for excellent fitness, and recommends going with an expert mountain guide. For a first ferrata, a guided day is the sensible format, and what a good private guide adds is most obvious on exposed ground.
⬡

Matching Format to Fitness

South Tyrol's tourism board advises being fit for 4 to 7 hours of walking a day on any of the high routes. The northern Alta Via 1 suits regular hikers new to hut-to-hut; the Alta Via 2 suits walkers comfortable with exposure and scrambling; the base-camp week suits groups whose fitness varies, because each person can choose the day's length.
When to Go

Dolomites by Season

Early Season
June
Huts open through June, many around the 20th, the start of the traditional CAI season. Snow can linger on north-facing passes and some huts are not yet staffed, so early-June departures need hut-by-hut checks.
Hut Season
Late June – September
The window Infodolomiti recommends for the Alta Via 1, with the route's huts open. July and August are the busiest months and bring afternoon thunderstorms, so stages start early. September is quieter, and the case for it is in the case for shoulder season.

Closing
Late September – October
The traditional season ends around 20 September. CAI's magazine Lo Scarpone reports more huts now opening from late May and closing in October, but dates vary by hut, so each one needs confirming.
Winter
December – April
Ski season. Dolomiti Superski covers 12 ski areas, about 450 lifts and 1,200 km of slopes on one pass, including Cortina, Val Gardena and Alta Badia. The summer trekking huts are closed.

The full route takes 10 to 15 days in 12 stages; the popular northern half takes about 5 to 7. The Cortina tourism board's day plan reaches Rifugio Croda da Lago on day 5, from where walkers either continue south toward the Civetta and Belluno or descend to Cortina.

Fly into Innsbruck or Venice for the start of the Alta Via 1, and out of Venice at the end.
