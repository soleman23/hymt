The Two Coasts

The same country, two entirely different weeks.

The Riviera Maya runs south from Cancún along the Caribbean: flat, hot, jungle-backed, with water that genuinely is the colour of the brochures. It is also the most overbuilt coastline in Mexico, which means the gap between an excellent property and a disappointing one is enormous and almost invisible from a booking site. Tulum in particular has changed beyond recognition in five years, and not always for the better.

Los Cabos sits at the tip of Baja, where the desert falls into the Pacific. The landscape is dramatic rather than pretty, the water is frequently not swimmable, and the appeal is elsewhere: the golf, the whale season, the sportfishing, and a dining scene that now justifies the trip on its own. It suits travellers who want a resort week with texture, and disappoints anyone expecting Caribbean calm.

The Details

What We Add to Mexico Planning

This is the coastline where good planning saves a holiday most often, because the failure modes are invisible in advance.

⬡
The Seaweed Question
Sargassum season runs roughly April to October and affects the Riviera Maya coast unevenly — some beaches badly, others barely. It is the single most common reason a Mexico trip disappoints, and it is entirely avoidable with the right timing and the right stretch of sand.
⬡
Swimmable or Not
Large parts of the Los Cabos coastline have dangerous surf and no safe swimming. Resorts do not advertise this. We book by beach first and property second, which is the reverse of how most Cabo trips are planned.
⬡
Property Reality
The Riviera Maya has more new-build luxury than anywhere in the Americas and the quality range is severe. We have stayed in them. The recommendation is based on the current state of the property, not the brochure or the year it opened.
⬡
Beyond the Resort
Private cenote access before the tour buses, archaeologists rather than guides at the Mayan sites, and the restaurants that locals book. The difference between a Mexico week and a Mexico week worth remembering.
When to Go

Mexico by Season

The two coasts run on different calendars, which is useful — there is almost always one of them at its best.

The Sweet Spot
November – April
Dry, warm, and clear on both coasts. Minimal sargassum risk on the Caribbean side, whale season in Baja from January. The obvious window, priced accordingly.
Best Window
Shoulder
May & October
Warmer and quieter, with real value at properties that are otherwise difficult. Sargassum becomes a factor on the Riviera Maya — worth taking advice on the specific beach before booking.
Summer
June – September
Hot, humid and wet on the Caribbean coast, with hurricane season overlapping. Los Cabos holds up better. For flexible travellers the pricing is genuinely compelling.
