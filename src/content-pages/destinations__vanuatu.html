Organised Around Visitors.

Vanuatu's dry season runs May through October, outside the November-to-April cyclone window. A first trip runs 9 to 12 days across two islands, not four. Espiritu Santo holds the SS President Coolidge, a 199-metre liner sunk in 1942 and entered from the beach; Tanna holds Mount Yasur, where crater access follows a published alert level.

Vanuatu is a chain of 83 islands, 65 of them inhabited, according to the Vanuatu Tourism Office. The country became independent on 30 July 1980 after seventy-four years as a joint British and French condominium, which is why English, French and Bislama are all official. It is not a resort destination and does not pretend to be. What it has instead is a shipwreck you walk to from a beach, a volcano you stand above at night, and islands where tourism is a sideline rather than the economy.

Espiritu Santo holds the SS President Coolidge, a 199-metre liner requisitioned as a troopship and lost on 26 October 1942 when she struck friendly mines entering the Segond Channel. Her master ran her onto the reef and more than five thousand men walked off; two did not. She lies on her port side with the bow near 20 metres and the stern past 60, close enough to shore that the dive begins on a beach rather than a boat. Nothing else anywhere matches that ratio of scale to access.

Tanna holds Mount Yasur, a 361-metre cone that has erupted more or less continuously since before Cook recorded its glow in 1774. You cross the ash plain by 4WD and walk the last stretch to a rim above live vents. The constraint on all of it is air. The domestic network runs small turboprops on thin schedules, weather cancels sectors, and a trip planned as though those flights were trains will lose days. Anyone who wants an overwater villa and one flight should book Fiji, and I will say so on the first call.

The Solomons and Papua New Guinea
The specialist trip, and the one I brief hardest before anyone commits. Start in Honiara for the Guadalcanal battlefields and the Bonegi shore wrecks, then fly west to Marovo Lagoon or Uepi for four nights of reef diving with a village-owned operator. From there, Papua New Guinea as a separate leg rather than a side trip: the Sepik for carving villages reached by river, Tufi for the fjord coast and its wrecks, or a Highlands cultural show if the dates line up. What We Add to a Melanesia Trip

This is the part of the Pacific where planning is not a luxury. Flights are thin, access rules move, and the difference between a good operator and an available one is not visible from a booking page.

⬡
The Schedule Is the Itinerary
Domestic air in Vanuatu runs small turboprops out of Bauerfield on schedules that change seasonally and cancel for weather. Everything else follows from that. We build the ground around the published air schedule, never book a domestic sector on the same day as an international departure, and put a night in Port Vila at each end of the trip as absorption. It reads like a wasted day on paper, and it is the only reason a cancelled sector costs an afternoon instead of the volcano. Most self-planned trips here fail on exactly this point.
⬡
Volcano Access Is a Live Rule
Yasur is not a booking, it is a permission that depends on the day. The Vanuatu Meteorology and Geo-hazards Department publishes a volcanic alert level for the volcano on a 0-to-5 scale, and access to the summit area is restricted as that level rises. Operators on Tanna work to it, and the good ones tell you before you fly rather than after you arrive. That is why we hold a second evening on Tanna rather than one, and why the itinerary says in writing what the alternative is if the rim is shut that week.
⬡
Dive Operator, Not Dive Site
The Coolidge is a technical dive dressed as a recreational one. The bow sits inside open-water limits; the engine room, the porcelain relief divers call the Lady and the cargo holds do not, and the profiles that reach them carry decompression obligations on every dive of the day. Operator selection decides whether that is managed properly or sold loosely to whoever turns up. We ask what gas is available, what the guide-to-diver ratio is inside the ship, how many dives a day the plan assumes, and what the nearest chamber and evacuation route actually are before anyone books.
⬡
The Honest Brief
Half of this job is telling people that this is not their trip. Vanuatu and the arc beyond it suit divers, walkers and travellers who find a cancelled flight interesting rather than ruinous. They do not suit a first Pacific holiday built around a spa, a kids' club and a villa over water — that trip exists, it is in Fiji or French Polynesia, and sending someone here instead produces a bad two weeks and a fair complaint. We ask that question on the first call rather than the fourth, because the answer changes the country, not the hotel.
When to Go

Vanuatu and the Arc by Season

Dry Season
May – October
The window, and it holds across the whole arc. South-east trades keep the humidity down, underwater visibility is at its best, and the months sit entirely outside the cyclone season. It is also when the humpbacks are in Tongan waters, roughly July through October, with August and September the most reliable weeks. The cost is that this is peak: Tonga's licensed whale boats and the better Espiritu Santo dive slots commit months out, and June and July nights on Tanna are genuinely cool at the rim.
Best Overall Window
Wet and Cyclone Season
November – April
The South Pacific cyclone season runs November to April, and it is not a formality here. Vanuatu sits in the cyclone belt and on the Ring of Fire, and Port Vila has taken damage from both cyclones and earthquakes in recent years. Rates fall and the islands are green. Diving on Santo continues, because the Coolidge lies in sheltered water. Travel in this window with flexible fares, real insurance, and a trip built so that a lost day is inconvenient rather than fatal.
The Shoulder Edges
April – May & October – November
The two seams, and the best value on this page. April and May catch the end of the wet with the islands green and the water warming; October and November get ahead of the cyclone window while whale season is still running in Tonga. Rates sit below the July peak and the dive boats are not full. The trade is variability: one of these weeks can be flat calm and the next can cancel a sector, which is exactly why the spare day belongs in this window.

Fiji has the highest density of private-island resorts anywhere, short transfers from an international gateway, and service built for people who want the day handled.
