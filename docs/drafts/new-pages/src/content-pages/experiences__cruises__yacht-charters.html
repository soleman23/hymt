Why Charter

The Whole Boat, Not a Cabin on It

A crewed charter hires the whole boat and its crew for one group, usually by the week. You pay the charter fee plus an Advance Provisioning Allowance, a percentage set in the contract, which the captain spends on fuel, food, drink and berths and accounts for with receipts before you leave. Caribbean charters run outside NOAA's 1 June–30 November hurricane season.

The captain proposes the day's run each morning, you decide where lunch is anchored, and the plan bends to the wind and to the cruising area in the contract. Nobody is aboard but your group and the crew.

It suits groups that can fill every cabin: friends, families, three generations on one deck (see multigenerational travel), and birthdays, anniversaries and proposals, which sit under romance and celebration travel.

It is the wrong format for a couple on their own: the fee is for the whole boat whether two people sail or ten, so a cabin on a small ship is the better buy, and the other cruise styles we plan cover it. It is also wrong for anyone who needs a fixed port schedule, or who is badly prone to motion sickness, because a yacht moves at anchor in a way a large stabilised ship does not.

How It Works

How a Crewed Charter Is Contracted and Paid

Four things decide the week before anyone steps aboard: the crew, the contract, the APA and what the rate leaves out.

⬡
The Crew
A captain runs the boat and the day, a chef cooks to the preference sheet you send ahead, and a deckhand or stewardess handles the tender, cabins and table. Under MYBA's guidance for captains and crew, the captain answers directly to you for how the provisioning money is spent.
⬡
The Contract
In the Mediterranean the usual form is the MYBA Charter Agreement. It fixes the charter period, cruising area, fee and payment dates, APA, security deposit and cancellation terms. Caribbean charters are often written on the CYBA E-Contract, drafted for inclusive charters on fully crewed yachts.
⬡
The APA
The Advance Provisioning Allowance is paid with the charter fee and covers your stay aboard: in practice fuel, food, drink, berths and port fees. The captain keeps you told of the balance, and you top it up if it runs low. Before you disembark, the MYBA terms have the captain present a statement with receipts and repay what is left.
⬡
Base Rate and Extras
The weekly rate pays for the boat and crew. Outside it sit the APA, a delivery fee if the boat comes to you from its base, VAT where the charter is taxed, and the gratuity. In the EU a vessel hired for 90 days or less is taxed where it is put at your disposal, under the VAT Directive's place-of-supply rules.
The Boats

Gulet, Catamaran or Motor Yacht

Three hulls, three kinds of week. The group and the coastline choose the boat.

⬡
Gulet
A wooden motor-sailer from Türkiye's coast. GoTürkiye puts the average at 70 to 80 feet, with a broad beam, two masts and four to six cabins. It motors more than it sails, and the wide aft deck is built for a long table. More in the gulet section.
⬡
Sailing or Power Catamaran
Two hulls, so it sits level at anchor and draws little water, which opens beach anchorages a deep-keeled yacht has to stand off from. The Greek islands and the Caribbean suit it.
⬡
Motor Yacht
Speed and range: more coast per day, and two islands in one day become realistic. The cost is fuel, drawn from the APA, so ask for the cruising speed and fuel burn alongside the rate.
⬡
How Many Can Sail
Under the Red Ensign Group Yacht Code, a commercially operated yacht of 24 metres and over carries no more than 12 passengers. Other flags set their own rules, so the boat's certificate, not the brochure, sets the number.

The Details

What We Handle on a Charter

The boat is the easy part. These are the pieces that decide the week.

⬡
Charter Brokers
Crewed charters are typically contracted through a charter broker on a standard agreement: the MYBA Charter Agreement in the Mediterranean, the CYBA E-Contract in the Caribbean. MYBA and CYBA publish the standard contracts their member brokers use.
⬡
Crew and the Preference Sheet
Crews change between seasons, so this year's captain and chef matter more than the boat's old reviews. We ask about both, and get your preference sheet to the chef before the provisioning is bought.
⬡
The Contract, Read With You
Before the deposit goes, we walk through the terms with you: payment dates, the cancellation scale, the cruising area, the security deposit and how the APA is set.
⬡
Embarkation Day
Flights timed to the boarding hour, the marina transfer, a hotel night first if the flight lands late, and soft bags the crew can stow.
⬡
Gratuity Guidance
MYBA's guidance says brokers generally suggest 5–15% of the gross charter fee for excellent service, at your discretion. You have the figure before the trip.

What It Costs

What a Private Charter Actually Costs

Priced per boat per week, not per person
A crewed charter is quoted for the whole boat and crew by the week, and the APA and the gratuity are then worked out as shares of that fee, so a per-person band would move with every guest added to the same boat and describe none of them.
What actually sets the number
  • Boat type, length and season, with the guest limit on the certificate: 12 for a commercial yacht of 24 metres and over under the Red Ensign Group Yacht Code
  • The APA, a percentage of the fee set in each contract and reconciled against receipts before disembarkation
  • Where the boat is put at your disposal: in the EU a hire of 90 days or less is taxed there, so the VAT differs between Greece, Italy and Croatia
  • Crew gratuity, which MYBA guidance puts at 5–15% of the gross charter fee, at the charterer's discretion

Tell me the dates and the party and you get a real quote, not a range. Source: MYBA guidelines for charter yacht captains & crew.
