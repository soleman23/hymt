Why Tennis Travel

How to Get a Good Seat
at a Grand Slam

A good Grand Slam seat comes one of three ways: a premium seat you own or lease (a Wimbledon debenture, a Roland-Garros loge), official hospitality, or the public ballot or sale. At Wimbledon the difference is legal. The AELTC issued 2,520 Centre Court debentures for the five Championships of 2026–2030, and debenture tickets are the only Wimbledon tickets that may be resold.

Below, the routes sit side by side, which none of the tournaments' own sites does. The trip around the seat, from the hotel to the nights either side, is ours to plan.

This is for travellers who know which Slam they want, can fix dates six to twelve months out, and would rather pay for a known seat than gamble on a draw. It is not for anyone who wants a named court on a named day for less than a debenture ticket, or who will not commit before the draw is out.

Ballot, Queue and resale tickets are personal to the buyer, so a visitor who wants a named Centre Court day is choosing between a debenture ticket and an Official Hospitality package. Debenture holders may sell a day they cannot use, which is why single-day debenture seats exist; read the AELTC's debentures page before paying anyone for one.

Enter the ballot alongside a paid route, not instead of one: it does not let you choose your day.

The grounds are a 15-minute walk from Southfields station on the District line, according to the AELTC's travel help page, and 20 minutes from Wimbledon station. For where to base yourself, see London, done properly around the fortnight.

The FFT's nearest equivalent to a debenture is the loge, sold for the fortnight or by the day. Its travel packages are the only route here that sells ticket and hotel together. The FFT warns that any 2027 individual ticket offered before its own sale opens is a scam; read its warning before paying anyone else.

Common Questions

Grand Slam Tennis Travel — FAQ

Debentures, ballots, queues, resale and when to go to Wimbledon, Roland-Garros and the Australian Open.

A debenture is a five-year right to one Centre Court or No.1 Court seat, and its holder may legally sell individual days. The current Centre Court series covers 2026–2030: 2,520 debentures at £116,000 each, per the AELTC's issue notice. Tickets printed "Debenture" in place of a price are the only Wimbledon tickets that may be resold, which is how a visitor buys a single day.

It is a random draw for up to two tickets per household, and the 2027 ballot ran 1–14 September 2026. Entry buys a place in the draw, not a ticket. The day, court and seat are allocated automatically, and successful applicants hear by email from October 2026, per the AELTC ballot page. Ballot tickets cannot be resold, so a planned trip cannot rest on one.

It is real but narrow: one ticket per person, a limited show-court allocation, and no Centre Court tickets on the last four days. Each arrival gets a numbered Queue Card in Wimbledon Park, and entry is in card order. Overnight queuers may use two-person tents only, per the AELTC Guide to Queueing. It suits a flexible traveller with a spare day, not a group needing seats together.

Official Hospitality packages a match ticket with hosted dining through Keith Prowse; a debenture ticket is a seat plus access to the debenture restaurants. Keith Prowse is the AELTC's exclusive Official Hospitality Partner, per the AELTC hospitality page. In the debenture areas, food and drink are bought on the day. The choice comes down to whether you want the day hosted or just the seat.

Only through each tournament's own channel, and at Wimbledon only for debenture tickets. The AELTC treats every other Wimbledon ticket as non-transferable and runs its own resale on site. Roland-Garros resale runs only through the official ticketing site. Australian Open tickets may be resold only through Ticketmaster Resale, under the AO 2027 conditions of sale. Tennis Australia can cancel a ticket resold any other way.

The Australian Open runs 11–31 January 2027 and Roland-Garros 17 May to 6 June 2027. Go in the first main-draw week for volume, the second for the big matches. The main draws open on 17 January in Melbourne and 23 May in Paris, per Tennis Australia and the FFT. Early rounds fill every court; by the quarter-finals eight singles matches remain across both draws.

Stay on a direct line: the District line for Wimbledon, Metro line 10 for Roland-Garros, the Route 70 tram for Melbourne Park. Southfields station is a 15-minute walk from the All England Club, and Porte d'Auteuil is a minute from the Auteuil entrance, per the FFT. Flinders Street is a 10-minute walk from Melbourne Park, per Tennis Australia.

Not in the debenture restaurants: the AELTC does not require men to wear a jacket or tie there. Many guests dress up anyway, and the AELTC advises dressing comfortably for British weather, per the 2026 Debenture Handbook. A hospitality provider may set its own standard, so check the package terms. Wear shoes you can walk in: Southfields station alone is 15 minutes from the gates.
