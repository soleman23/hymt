A real destination for every experience tile

Where each of the 126 tiles on the 12 experience pages should link, which existing pages carry that weight, the few new pages worth building, and the order to ship it in. Planning only: nothing on the site has changed.

9new pages recommended, plus 5 that depend on owner decisions

Recommendation Link the 78 sub-experience cards to existing pages first, and build new pages only where nothing on the site answers the click. 62 of the 78 resolve to an existing destination page, hub or journal post, with no new page needed. 25 of those cards need a targeted enhancement (one FAQ, one itinerary card, a corrected season), not a new section. Keep the 48 itinerary cards' "Plan This Trip" buttons on the form, add a ?type= pre-fill where the form supports one, and add at most one "in detail →" text link per card.

Build 9 new pages: Orlando (Disney World and Universal), St. Lucia and the Dolomites as destination pages, and six nested experience-detail pages: Tennis, Formula 1 and Golf under Sports & Event Travel, and Private Yacht Charters, European River Cruises and Mediterranean Small-Ship Cruises under Cruises. Five more tiles (Namibia, Disneyland, interior Mexico, the American Southwest, Football & Rugby) wait on whether HYMT actually sells those trips.

Ship it through one tile map (src/data/tiles.mjs) that both the build and the destination pages read. The map rewrites the card links and generates a "Featured in" strip back to each experience page. Four new verifier checks keep a card from ever pointing back at the form by accident.

Why this shape

  • Every tile gets a real page, but no new URL competes with an existing one. The page audits proposed 12 new URLs and floated more (Croatia, Turkey, a horse-racing page, per-event pages, an Alps ski page). Consolidation kept 9, gated 4 on owner decisions and rejected the rest; §10 lists each with its reason.
  • The reuse work is small and safe. It changes hrefs and adds short modules on pages that already rank for the subject, with word budgets checked against the 3,500-word ceiling.
  • New pages go only where a visitor's question has no answer on the site today: which Orlando park, a St. Lucia-only week, a Dolomites hut trek, how a Wimbledon debenture works, what a crewed charter costs.

How this plan was built

12 parallel page audits (one per experience page, each reading the page source and every candidate target's source), one independent architecture read of the layouts, schema and verifier, and one site-wide consolidation pass that collapsed the 126 tile rows into 56 subjects with one canonical URL each. Every decision except the 22 high-confidence unchanged reuses then went through two adversarial reviewers, one on duplication and SEO and one on whether the click delivers what the tile promises. 0 of 34 decisions were refuted; 28 were upheld with changes, and those changes are applied throughout this plan. Each of the 13 new or conditional pages then got a full content brief.

02Current-state architecture

Verified against HEAD 5f547a7 and production on 2 Oct 2026.

How the pages are assembled

  • Every /experiences/<slug>/ page is two files: src/pages/experiences/<slug>/index.astro (title, description, name, hero props) wrapping src/content-pages/experiences__<slug>.html, imported ?raw. ExperienceLayout.astro owns a hardcoded three-crumb trail (Home › Experiences › Name), the hero, and the JSON-LD: BreadcrumbList, WebPage, Service, and FAQPage parsed from the body.
  • Every /destinations/<slug>/ page has the same shape on DestinationLayout.astro, plus a region prop that adds the fourth crumb on the 59 country pages; the 9 regional hubs omit it. Schema is BreadcrumbList, WebPage, TouristDestination and FAQPage. The hero stat rail must carry "Best Season" and "Best For".
  • The /experiences/ and /destinations/ hubs are hand-written card HTML on Base.astro, with a CollectionPage ItemList built from a depth-1 glob. A nested route such as /experiences/cruises/yacht-charters/ joins neither ItemList and does not change the "12 trip types" count that public/llms.txt states and the verifier enforces.
  • Canonical, og:url and every JSON-LD URL derive from astro.config.mjs → site, and canonicals drop the query string, so /plan-your-trip/?type=safari is invisible to search.

The tiles today

  • 78 .exp-card anchors in an .exp-cards--photo grid under section#destinations: 6 per page, 9 on Family and Food & Wine. The card is the link, so nothing inside it may be a link (verifier nested-card-anchor).
  • 48 .event-card "Trips We Plan Often" cards (4 per page), each with an a.event-cta "Plan This Trip" button. The card itself is a div.
  • All 126 point at /plan-your-trip/. There is no tile map; targets are 126 literal hrefs. The "Explore →" arrow on every card promises a page it does not deliver.
  • Same pattern elsewhere, out of scope here: 330 of 386 .place-cards on destination pages and 248 .itin-cta buttons also point at the form. The data model below is sized so the same mechanism can serve them later.

Reverse links

Only 3 of 68 destination pages link to any experience page (Costa Rica, Dominican Republic, Zambia & Victoria Falls). Each destination page has one stable anchor, id="places". Maldives has id="experiences" instead and a different section set. Neither id="itineraries" nor any per-section id exists yet.

Existing data patterns to copy

tools/cost-ranges-data.mjs plus tools/p3-8-cost-insert.mjs (a keyed data file and an idempotent script that rewrites a marked section) is the established pattern for data-driven content without a component refactor. tools/p3-6-related-destinations.mjs is a second example.

Build gates that constrain this work

npm run build runs 63 named verifier checks. The ones this project touches: internal-links (every same-site href must resolve, though it strips fragments), nested-card-anchor, photo-grid, internal-link-floor (≥2 journal links per destination or experience page), page-length (3,500 body words on 4-crumb destination pages), head-baseline, schema-required, sitemap-parity, llms-txt counts, and the image attribute and ratio checks. Rebuilding an unchanged tree leaves dist/ byte-identical, so the regression test for any layout change is an empty git diff --stat dist/.

Production vs source

Production matches source for everything this plan touches (the sitemap's 122 URLs are identical). The only drift is documentary: README.md still says "43 destination pages" and that production is not on this host. That is a docs chore outside this project.

03Key findings and risks

Verified Findings

  1. Every tile on every experience page dead-ends at the form. All 126, confirmed in source, dist and production. The "Explore →" arrow is a broken promise on 78 cards.
  2. Most tiles already have a strong page. 41 subjects (62 of the 78 cards) map to an existing page with fit 2–3 out of 3: the page is about the subject, or a full section covers it.
  3. Fragment links are not checked today. The dead-link check strips #fragment, so /destinations/maldives/#places would ship green although Maldives has no such id. The plan adds a fragment check before any deep link ships.
  4. Word ceilings are tight. 27 country pages have under 200 words of headroom. The five tightest have 62–68: Arctic Norway, Israel, Colombia, Dominican Republic and UAE & Gulf. Any reverse link on a tight page is wrapped on existing words, and any layout-rendered module must be excluded from bodyWords.
  5. Tile copy contradicts its target page in several places. Family Safari calls Sabi Sand "malaria-free", against the CDC-cited South Africa page. Other examples: the Seychelles, St. Barth's, Sri Lanka and Bali seasons; the Peru acclimatization order; the Sunrise Seto train (it terminates at Takamatsu, not Matsuyama). Linking a tile one click from its contradiction makes this worse, so these copy fixes ship before the links.
  6. Hub card tags disagree with the tiles on 10 of 12 experience hub cards. Examples: the Wellness card names Switzerland and Thailand, which have no tile; the Adventure card names Verbier and the Coral Triangle, which no page covers.
  7. Thin reverse linking. 3 of 68 destination pages link to any experience page, and Family and Multigenerational sell the same Italy and Hawaii trips with no link between them.
  8. The form's ?type= pre-fill works for exactly six values: beach, cruise, family, group, honeymoon, safari (plan-your-trip.html line 358, kebab-case validated).

05Routing rules

Recommendation Apply these in order; the first rule that fits decides. Every rule names one canonical URL. No sub-experience card links to /plan-your-trip/ once its target exists, nor to a query-string variant, nor to a page that exists only to receive the tile.

  1. R1 · Exact destination match. The tile names a place with its own page, and that page's intro, places grid, itineraries or FAQ serves the tile at fit ≥2 → REUSE. Maldives → /destinations/maldives/.
  2. R2 · One destination under several experiences. One canonical URL regardless of how many experience pages feature it. The tile copy carries the intent; the destination carries one "Featured in" strip and, where the audit found a real gap, one targeted FAQ or card, never a section per experience. Japan serves 5 tiles on 5 pages.
  3. R3 · Combined tiles ("A & B"). (a) One established product covered by one page → reuse and keep the label: Bora Bora & French Polynesia. (b) One half is a modifier → reuse the subject's page and rename the tile if the modifier over-promises: "Jordan & the Levant" → Jordan. (c) Two separately booked places with coverage for only one → link the stronger half and drop or add the other: "St. Lucia & Anguilla" → St. Lucia. A combination page only when the pairing is sold as one trip and nothing covers either half. None qualified.
  4. R4 · Regional tile with a hub. If the intent is "pick somewhere in this region", link the hub: Caribbean → /destinations/caribbean-mexico/#places; Antarctica & the Arctic → /destinations/polar-regions/. If the tile is a format (river cruising), the hub is the wrong answer; see R5.
  5. R5 · Thematic tile (sport, cruise format, charter). Prefer an existing commercial page where the theme is the place's core (Galápagos and Alaska for cruises). A journal post is the canonical target only when it is the site's most complete answer and nothing commercial is warranted (Willamette, Horse Racing, Heli-Skiing). Otherwise, with distinct search demand, create an experience-detail page nested under the parent, one page per sport or format, never one per event.
  6. R6 · Destination + travel style ("Family Safari", "A Safari Honeymoon", "Kyoto in Season"). The destination page or region hub is canonical; the style lives in tile copy plus one targeted FAQ. Never create a /destinations/<place>-<style>/ page.
  7. R7 · Major attractions (Walt Disney World, Universal, Disneyland). One destination page per resort area, with stable section ids for each resort, beats a page per park: Disney World and Universal share /destinations/orlando/.
  8. R8 · Tile broader or narrower than the page. Link the page whose subject matches the tile's main noun; rename the tile if its label over-promises ("Italy & Europe" → Italy). A narrower tile (Santorini, Amalfi) links the country page without a fragment unless a stable section answers it on its own.
  9. R9 · Deep links. Only to a stable section that answers the tile by itself: #places, the new sitewide #itineraries, or a named section on an approved new page. An id is never invented for one tile alone, and every fragment is checked at build time.
  10. R10 · Competing pages. Before any CREATE, grep src/content-pages/ for the subject; if an existing page scores fit ≥2, enhance it instead. Two pages may not share a primary subject. A nested detail page goes one level more specific than its parent (venues, formats, calendars, costs) and never restates the parent's intro.

11Template and data architecture

Templates

Recommendation

  • Existing destination pages: DestinationLayout unchanged for routing, plus one optional generated module, "Featured in".
  • New destination pages: DestinationLayout with a region prop. Model page: destinations__turks-caicos.html.
  • New experience-detail pages: ExperienceLayout with one new optional prop, parent?: { name: string; path: string }, which mirrors DestinationLayout's region. With it, the visible trail and the BreadcrumbList both read Home › Experiences › Sports & Event Travel › Tennis. Without it, output is byte-identical to today. The verifier already requires ExperienceLayout recursively under src/pages/experiences/; schema-required keys on the prefix, so Service schema is emitted and required; and the depth-1 count keeps "12 trip types" true.
  • Section mix for a detail page, existing classes only: intro-section with answer capsule → exp-cards-section (venues or events; swatch grid until every card has a real photo) → events-section → inclusions-section → seasons-strip (the calendar) → exp-cta → cost-range (a row in cost-ranges-data.mjs) → page-faq → read-more (≥2 journal links). No new CSS beyond the two classes below.
  • Rejected: a new ExperienceDetailLayout, which would duplicate ~120 lines and a stylesheet for one crumb. DestinationLayout for thematic pages: wrong schema and the stat rail. JournalLayout: Article schema and a dated byline on an evergreen commercial page. Flat /experiences/formula-1-travel/: it joins the hub ItemList, breaks the llms.txt count and loses the parent crumb.

  • tools/tile-links-apply.mjs, idempotent and modelled on p3-8-cost-insert.mjs. It pairs each <a class="exp-card" href> with the next exp-card__name, refuses on any count or name mismatch, rewrites only the href (and the event-card CTA and its secondary link), and prints what it changed. --check exits non-zero on drift.

12SEO/AIO and duplicate-content strategy

  • One canonical per subject. 56 subjects, 56 URLs. Multiple tiles share a target (Japan ×5, Italy ×6, Greece ×4); the tiles carry the experience angle, and the destination page stays about the destination. Canonicals are self-referencing and inherited from Base.astro; nothing here overrides them.
  • No style-variant URLs. No /destinations/maldives-honeymoon/, no query variants, no per-experience sections on destination pages (R6).
  • Parent and child never compete. A nested page owns the specific query ("Wimbledon debenture tickets"), and the parent keeps the broad one ("sports event travel"). The parent's Monaco cost drivers and capsule clause move to the F1 page, leaving one clause on the parent that links down. The Masters report keeps the Augusta query; the golf page caps Augusta at ~120 words and links it.
  • Hub vs new page. At launch, the Barbados & Eastern Caribbean card for Soufrière and the Europe hub's river FAQ link down to the new pages, so the hubs defer to the new canonicals.
  • Schema per page type. Destination: WebPage + BreadcrumbList + TouristDestination + FAQPage. Experience detail: WebPage + BreadcrumbList (4 items) + Service + FAQPage. Journal: Article + BreadcrumbList. Never Offer, TouristTrip or Event on itinerary cards, and never AggregateRating.
  • AIO on every new page and every enhanced FAQ. A 40–70 word answer capsule under the first h2 with at least one number from a named source. FAQ first sentences answer in ≤25 words. Time-sensitive facts carry a visible "Checked <Month> 2026" date: Epic Universe, Typhoon Lagoon, gulet season, the Grand Slam ballots, the F1 calendar.
  • Titles and descriptions for new pages are drafted in each brief within 30–65 and 110–165 characters. Each new page's head-baseline entry is its own commit. No reuse in this plan changes an existing title or description.

13Internal-linking and breadcrumb strategy

  • Forward: tile → canonical page, generated from the map. Intro, seasons and FAQ copy on the experience page that names a tile subject links it once per section with descriptive anchor text ("Ayurvedic programmes in Sri Lanka"), never "Explore →" and never inside a card.
  • Reverse: the generated "Featured in" strip on every targeted destination page, plus one prose link from each enhanced page's new FAQ or itinerary, never inside a .place-card. On pages under 200 words of headroom, the link is wrapped on existing words.
  • Sideways: one body-copy link each way between Family and Multigenerational (they sell the same Italy and Hawaii trips). Each new detail page links up to its parent in body copy and across to the 2–4 destination pages it relies on.
  • Journal floor: unchanged (≥2 journal links per destination or experience page, ≥2 destination or experience links per post). Swap /travel-journal/maldives-overwater-vs-beach-villa/ into Beach & Island's Read More; it already links there and is not linked back.
  • Breadcrumbs never vary by referrer. A visitor from Romance lands on Home › Destinations › Asia › The Maldives; "Featured in" is the way back. Nested pages: Home › Experiences › Cruises › Private Yacht Charters.
  • Hubs: retag the /experiences/ hub cards to name the subjects each page's tiles actually carry (body copy, no head-baseline change). Make the destination hub headings count-free ("Destinations worth planning properly") in the same PR as the first new card, so a seventh card cannot go stale. Remove the stray "Anguilla" tag from destinations.html.

14Content and image requirements

Content

  • Copy fixes before links (P1, small): the malaria claim on the Family Safari tile, the Africa hub (4 statements) and the africa-destination-guide post, cited to the CDC; plus the season and fact corrections listed in §10 and §13.
  • Enhancements: 18 pages, ~30–250 words each, totals in §7. All FAQ additions follow the accordion rules (button in heading, aria-controls, role="region", answer visible without JS).

  • Journal gaps to commission later (each changes the "32 field reports" count): trekking, cruise, tennis and F1 field reports.
